How to store limes, and why they go bad

Limes are sensitive to cold and to ethylene. They yellow near ripening fruit, get brown leathery patches in the fridge, and dry out on the counter.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
2-3 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 54°F (12°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Hard, dry limesTurning yellowBrown, sunken patches on the peelSoft spot with green or blue mold

How to store limes

  • Store in the refrigerator in a sealed plastic bag
  • Keeps about a week on the counter
  • Microwave for 10 seconds before juicing to get more juice
  • Store cut limes tightly wrapped

Picking good limes: Firm with glossy, bright green skin. Heavier limes have more juice.

Does a Krisp help? Long baseline life and little ethylene response.

Why limes go bad

Hard, dry limes

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe peel turns hard and dull and the lime gives very little juice.

Why it happens

Limes lose water through the peel and keep best at 90 to 95 percent humidity. A fruit bowl is far drier than that. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if there's no mold. There is just less juice.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep limes in a sealed bag in the fridge
Use it up
  • Microwave it for 10 seconds, roll it, then juice it
  • Zest it and mix the zest with salt

Turning yellow

Ethylene gasStill good

What you'll seeThe green peel fades to yellow-green, then yellow.

Why it happens

Ethylene breaks down the green pigment in lime peel and lets the yellow underneath show. Apples, bananas and other ripening fruit nearby are the usual source. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if it's firm and has no mold. The change is in the peel color.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep limes away from apples, bananas and tomatoes
  • Store them in a sealed bag in the fridge

Brown, sunken patches on the peel

Too coldStill good

What you'll seeSmall pits in the peel that merge into brown, leathery, sunken areas.

Why it happens

Limes get chilling injury below 50°F, and it gets worse the colder and longer they are stored. Pits form in the peel and can merge into brown, leathery patches. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the inside looks and smells normal. Juice it soon. Toss it if the patches are soft or moldy.

Prevent it next time
  • Buy limes in small amounts and use them within two to three weeks
  • For up to a week, the counter is fine
Use it up
  • Juice it and freeze the juice in an ice cube tray

Soft spot with green or blue mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft, watery patch that grows white fuzz and then green or blue powder.

Why it happens

Green mold and blue mold are the main lime diseases after harvest, along with rots that start at the stem end. Cuts and bruises in the peel let them in. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss a moldy lime. Citrus is soft, juicy fruit, so mold can spread below the surface. Check the fruit that was touching it.

Prevent it next time
  • Remove any soft lime from the bag as soon as you see it
  • Handle limes gently

What went wrong?

Limes and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Limes give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Limes are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

The part people throw away

About 16% of limes by weight is usually trimmed off as peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Zest before juicing and freeze it
  • Mix zest with salt for lime salt
