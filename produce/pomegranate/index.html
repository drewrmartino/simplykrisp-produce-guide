How to store pomegranates, and why they go bad

Pomegranates keep for a month or more in the fridge and don't ripen after harvest. They mostly dry out. The thing to watch for is rot inside, which the skin can hide.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
1-3 months (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 41°F (5°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular

How to store pomegranates

  • Store whole in the refrigerator for up to 3 months
  • Arils (seeds) can be stored in an airtight container for up to 5 days
  • Can also sit on the counter for 1-2 weeks
  • Freeze arils in a single layer for long-term storage

Picking good pomegranates: Heavy for its size with a deep color. The skin should be smooth and firm.

Does a Krisp help? Loss is water loss and husk scald, not ripening.

Why pomegranates go bad

Hard, shrunken, leathery skin

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe skin dries, hardens and pulls in around the arils.

Why it happens

Pomegranates are very prone to water loss, which shrivels the skin. 1

Is it still OK?

Usually still good. Open it: if the arils are plump and smell fresh, eat them.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep whole pomegranates in the fridge, in a bag
Use it up
  • Freeze the arils in a single layer
  • Juice them

Brown or black arils inside

MoldToss it

What you'll seeArils that are brown, black or mushy, sometimes with an off-color patch of skin outside.

Why it happens

Heart rot is a fungal infection that blackens the arils while the outside looks close to normal. Gray mold works from the outside in: the rind turns light brown and leathery, then gray fuzz appears. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it if the arils are black, mushy or moldy, or smell fermented.

Prevent it next time
  • Choose fruit that feels heavy, with no soft or discolored patches
  • Refrigerate pomegranates rather than leaving them out for weeks

Brown patches on the skin

Too coldStill good

What you'll seeBrown discoloration on the skin after a long time in cold storage.

Why it happens

After two months or more in the cold, pomegranate skin can turn brown. UC Davis describes husk scald, which stays in the skin, and chilling injury, which can also leave the arils pale and the white membranes brown. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the arils inside are firm, juicy and smell fresh, with no mold.

Prevent it next time
  • Use pomegranates within a month or two
  • Eat the oldest ones first
Use it up
  • Scatter the arils over salads or yogurt

What went wrong?

Pomegranates and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Pomegranates give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Pomegranates are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 44% of pomegranates by weight is usually trimmed off as peel and membrane (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Dry the peel and steep a small piece as a tart tea
  • Compost the membranes
