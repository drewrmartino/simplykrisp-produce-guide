How to store bagged salad, and why it goes bad

Bagged salad is already cut and washed, so it runs on its use-by date. Once the bag is open you have about two days.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
3-5 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 34°F (1°C)
Don't store next to
Apples and pears
What are you seeing?
Wet, slimy leavesBrown or pink cut edges

How to store bagged salad

  • Keep the bag or tub sealed and cold until you need it, and go by the use-by date
  • Once opened, eat it within about 2 days
  • Tuck a dry paper towel into the opened bag or tub to soak up moisture
  • Keep it off the back wall of the fridge, where the leaves can freeze

Picking good bagged salad: Pick the bag with the latest date, dry leaves and no dark, wet or slimy pieces.

Does a Krisp help? Bagged salad is cut, washed and sealed, so it runs on its use-by date. Once the bag is open, moisture and bruised edges end it in about two days, and a Krisp won't change that.

Why bagged salad goes bad

Wet, slimy leaves

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeLeaves turn dark, wet and slimy, and the bag smells sour.

Why it happens

Cut leaves leak moisture and break down faster than a whole head. USDA's FoodKeeper gives bagged greens 3 to 5 days past the date on the bag unopened, and about 2 days once it is open. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss the bag if the leaves are slimy or it smells sour. Picking out the bad pieces is not enough once slime has spread through the bag.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the bag cold and sealed until you need it
  • Tuck a dry paper towel into an opened bag or tub
  • Buy the bag with the latest date

Brown or pink cut edges

AgeStill good

What you'll seeThe cut edges of the lettuce turn brown or pink.

Why it happens

Lettuce browns where it was cut. Salad bags are packed with very little oxygen to slow this down, and it speeds up once the bag is opened. 3

Is it still OK?

Browning on cut edges is a quality problem, not a safety one. Eat it soon if the leaves are still crisp and smell fresh.

Prevent it next time
  • Use an opened bag within about 2 days
  • Press the air out and reseal the bag between uses
Use it up
  • Sauté mixed greens into eggs or pasta

Bagged salad and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Bagged salad gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Bagged salad is somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing it away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep it apart from apples and pears.
