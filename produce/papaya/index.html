How to store papaya, and why it goes bad

Papaya is damaged below about 54°F unless it's already ripe. Chilled too early, it pits, blotches and never ripens properly. Ripe, it has only a few days.

Where to keep it
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long it lasts
5-7 days ripe (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 54°F (12°C)
Don't store next to
Apples and bananas
What are you seeing?
Blotchy skin that never ripensBrown, sunken spots on the skinMushy, very soft flesh

How to store papaya

  • Ripen at room temperature until skin is mostly yellow
  • Refrigerate once ripe
  • Store cut papaya in an airtight container
  • Place in a paper bag with a banana to speed ripening

Picking good papaya: Mostly yellow to orange skin that gives slightly to pressure. A slight sweet aroma at the stem.

Does a Krisp help? Papayas ripen on the counter, and once ripe they have only a day or two. A Krisp adds little.

Why papaya goes bad

Blotchy skin that never ripens

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seePitted or blotchy skin, hard patches in the flesh, and fruit that stays partly green and bland.

Why it happens

Papaya gets chilling injury below about 54°F, and green fruit is hit hardest. The skin pits and blotches, the fruit ripens unevenly, and the flesh can stay hard or turn watery. It also decays more easily. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if the flesh smells fresh and has no mold. It will be bland.

Prevent it next time
  • Ripen papaya on the counter until the skin is mostly yellow
  • Refrigerate only ripe papaya
Use it up
  • Blend it into a smoothie with lime
  • Cook green, firm papaya in a curry or stir-fry

Brown, sunken spots on the skin

MoldTrim and use

What you'll seeSmall brown, water-soaked spots that grow as the fruit ripens, or a dark stem end.

Why it happens

Anthracnose is the main papaya disease after harvest. It starts as small, brown, shallow spots that can enlarge. Other fungi rot the stem end, turning it dark brown to black. 123

Is it still OK?

Shallow spots that stay in the skin come off when you peel it. If the flesh underneath is soft, dark or smells off, or you see fuzzy mold, toss the papaya.

Prevent it next time
  • Choose papayas without sunken spots or a dark, soft stem end
  • Eat ripe papaya promptly
Use it up
  • Peel it, check the flesh, and cube the good fruit for the freezer

Mushy, very soft flesh

OverripeningCook it soon

What you'll seeThe fruit gives easily all over and the flesh is very soft.

Why it happens

Papaya keeps ripening after harvest, softening and yellowing as it goes, and it does so faster when warm. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if it smells sweet. Toss it if it smells sour or fermented, or the flesh is slimy.

Prevent it next time
  • Move ripe papaya to the fridge
  • Store cut papaya in a covered container and eat it within a few days
Use it up
  • Blend it into a smoothie
  • Freeze cubes for later

What went wrong?

Papaya and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Papaya gives off a moderate amount of ethylene as it ripens, enough to speed up sensitive produce in a closed drawer.
  • Papaya is somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing it away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep it apart from apples and bananas.

The part people throw away

About 38% of papaya by weight is usually trimmed off as seeds and skin (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Dry the seeds and grind a little as a peppery seasoning
  • Compost the skin
