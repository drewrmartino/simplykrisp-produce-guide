Why it goes bad

Tomatoes: what went wrong, and what to do

Tomatoes are damaged by cold, keep ripening on the counter, and mold quickly once the skin splits. They also give off ethylene that hurts nearby greens.

Where to keep it
Counter
How long it lasts
5-7 days (counter)
Keep it away from
Cucumbers, Leafy greens and Peppers

Mealy, bland tomatoes

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeFlesh is soft and grainy, flavor is flat, and color may be blotchy.

Why it happens

Tomatoes are damaged by cold. Below 50°F for more than two weeks, or at 41°F for more than six to eight days, they lose flavor, soften early, ripen unevenly and rot more easily. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. The texture won't come back, so cook them.

Prevent it next time
  • Ripen and keep tomatoes on the counter, stem-side down
  • Move only fully ripe ones to the fridge, and only for a few days
Use it up
  • Simmer them into sauce or soup
  • Roast them with olive oil and garlic

Very soft and wrinkly

OverripeningCook it soon

What you'll seeTomatoes feel soft all over and the skin starts to wrinkle.

Why it happens

Tomatoes keep ripening on the counter, driven by their own ethylene, until they go past their best. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if there's no mold or leaking. Ideal for cooking.

Prevent it next time
  • Buy a mix of ripe and less-ripe tomatoes
  • Move fully ripe tomatoes to the fridge for a couple of days if you need more time
Use it up
  • Blend them into sauce, salsa or gazpacho
  • Save cores and skins in a freezer stock bag

Moldy, split or leaking

MoldToss it

What you'll seeFuzzy mold, a cracked skin with wet flesh, or a sour smell.

Why it happens

Several molds, including gray mold and black mold, attack tomatoes, often through cracks and damaged skin. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it. USDA lists tomatoes as soft produce, where mold can spread below the surface.

Prevent it next time
  • Use split tomatoes the same day
  • Keep tomatoes in a single layer so they don't crush each other

How to store tomatoes

  • Store stem-side down on the counter at room temperature
  • Never refrigerate unripe tomatoes — cold kills flavor and texture
  • Only refrigerate if very ripe and you need to buy time
  • Keep away from direct sunlight

Does a Krisp help? Tomatoes keep their flavor best on the counter, out of reach of a Krisp in the drawer. If you move ripe ones into the fridge to hold them a couple more days, a Krisp in that drawer helps a little.

Full storage details are in the Produce Storage Guide.

The part people throw away

About 9% of tomatoes by weight is usually trimmed off as cores and skins (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Save cores and skins in a freezer stock bag
  • Dry skins in a low oven and grind into tomato powder
