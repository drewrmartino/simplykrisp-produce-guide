How to store onions, and why they go bad

Whole onions keep for a month or more in a cool, dry, dark spot. Over time they sprout, pick up black mold under the skin, or rot from the neck down.

Where to keep them
Cool, dark pantry
How long they last
1-3 months (pantry)
Best temperature
About 45°F (7°C)
Don't store next to
Potatoes
What are you seeing?
Green sproutsBlack powder under the skinSoft, smelly layers

How to store onions

  • Store in a cool, dark, dry, well-ventilated area
  • Keep away from potatoes — they cause each other to spoil faster
  • Do not store in the refrigerator until cut
  • Cut onions should be refrigerated in an airtight container

Picking good onions: Firm with dry, papery outer skin and no soft spots or sprouting.

Does a Krisp help? Onions are a dry pantry item, and ethylene actually suppresses sprouting rather than causing it — it is sold commercially as a sprout inhibitor.

Why onions go bad

Green sprouts

AgeTrim and use

What you'll seeA green shoot grows from the top, and the onion feels softer than it did.

Why it happens

Onions sprout once their dormancy ends. Commercial storage holds them at 32°F, and UC Davis notes that anything from 41 to 77°F favors sprouting. That covers every spot in a house, so at home it comes down to time. 12

Is it still OK?

Utah State University Extension says to use sprouting onions first. If the onion is firm, with no mold, slime or bad smell, cut out the shoot and any soft layers and use the rest soon. Toss it if it's mushy or smells off.

Prevent it next time
  • Buy onions in amounts you'll use within a month or two
  • Keep them cool, dry and in the dark, with air around them
Use it up
  • Cook it the same week. Sprouted onions keep getting softer

Black powder under the skin

MoldTrim and use

What you'll seeBlack, sooty powder on or under the papery skin, usually near the neck.

Why it happens

This is black mold. It shows up as black discoloration at the neck and on the outer layers. 13

Is it still OK?

Onions are firm, so USDA says small mold spots can be cut off with at least an inch around and below them. Peel off every layer that has black powder on it and rinse what is left. If the mold runs between the inner layers, or the onion is soft or smells off, toss it.

Prevent it next time
  • Store onions dry, with air around them, not in a plastic bag
  • Use onions with damaged or loose skins first
Use it up
  • Cook the cleaned onion the same day

Soft, smelly layers

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeSoft, watery or slimy layers with a sour or foul smell, sometimes only on the inside or at the neck.

Why it happens

Several rots do this. Bacterial soft rot is watery and foul-smelling, sour skin turns inner layers slimy and yellow-brown, and neck rot starts at the neck and moves down through the bulb. 14

Is it still OK?

Toss it. FDA says to throw away produce that looks rotten. Check the onions stored next to it.

Prevent it next time
  • Choose onions with tight, dry necks and no soft spots
  • Keep them dry

What went wrong?

Onions and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Onions give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Onions are barely affected by ethylene.

Keep them apart from potatoes.

The part people throw away

About 10% of onions by weight is usually trimmed off as skins and ends (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Add skins to stock for color and flavor
  • Simmer skins for a natural golden dye
