How to store asparagus, and why it goes bad

Asparagus keeps growing after it's cut, which is why it goes downhill so fast. The spears turn woody, go limp, or get slimy at the tips, usually within a week.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
3-5 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 36°F (2°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, avocados and peaches
What are you seeing?
Tough, woody spearsLimp, bendy spearsDark, slimy tipsTips opening up

How to store asparagus

  • Trim the ends and stand upright in a jar with 1 inch of water
  • Cover loosely with a plastic bag
  • Or wrap ends in a damp paper towel
  • Use as soon as possible for best flavor

Picking good asparagus: Choose firm, bright green stalks with tight, compact tips.

Why asparagus goes bad

Tough, woody spears

Ethylene gasCook it soon

What you'll seeSpears are tough and stringy, especially toward the cut end.

Why it happens

Asparagus builds woody fiber after harvest. UC Davis notes that ethylene speeds this toughening up, and that includes the ethylene bruised spears make themselves. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. Snap or cut off the tough ends and peel the lower stalks.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep asparagus away from apples, avocados and peaches
  • Handle the bunch gently
  • Cook it within three to five days
Use it up
  • Peel the lower stalks and roast or grill them
  • Simmer the tough ends into soup and blend it
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp helps a little here. Slows the spears turning tough and woody. Expect about 1–2 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Limp, bendy spears

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeSpears bend instead of snapping, and the cut ends look dry and shrunken.

Why it happens

Asparagus dries out fast. UC Davis recommends 95 to 100 percent humidity and counts dried-out ends as a quality defect. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the tips aren't slimy and it smells fresh.

Prevent it next time
  • Stand the bunch upright in a jar with an inch of water and cover it loosely with a bag
  • Or wrap the cut ends in a damp paper towel
Use it up
  • Trim the ends and stand the spears in cold water for an hour
  • Roast or sauté them

Dark, slimy tips

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeThe tips turn dark, mushy and slimy, and smell bad.

Why it happens

This is bacterial soft rot. UC Davis notes that it can start at the tips or at the cut end. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss slimy or bad-smelling spears. FDA says to throw away produce that looks rotten. Check the rest of the bunch, and cook any firm, fresh-smelling spears today.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the tips dry. Only the cut ends should sit in water
  • Change the water every day or two
  • Use asparagus within a few days

Tips opening up

AgeStill good

What you'll seeThe tight tips loosen and spread open, and the spears bend upward.

Why it happens

Asparagus keeps growing after it's cut. The tips open up, which growers call feathering, and the spears bend away from gravity. It happens quickly above 50°F. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. It's a sign of age, so cook it soon.

Prevent it next time
  • Refrigerate asparagus as soon as you get home
  • Store it upright
Use it up
  • Roast, grill or sauté it

What went wrong?

Asparagus and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Asparagus gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Asparagus is somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing it away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep it apart from apples, avocados and peaches.

The part people throw away

About 47% of asparagus by weight is usually trimmed off as woody ends (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Simmer ends into stock, then blend and strain for asparagus soup
  • Peel the lower stalk and roast it with the tips
