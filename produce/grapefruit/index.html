How to store grapefruit, and why it goes bad

Grapefruit keeps best at 54 to 57°F, warmer than a fridge. Over a long cold stay the peel pits and browns. Mold and drying out are the other ways it goes.

How to store grapefruit

  • Store in the refrigerator for longest freshness
  • Can sit on the counter for about a week
  • No special storage requirements — quite hardy
  • Bring to room temperature before eating for best flavor

Picking good grapefruit: Heavy for its size with thin, smooth skin. Color does not indicate ripeness.

Does a Krisp help? Citrus is only moderately sensitive and keeps for weeks regardless — ethylene changes the peel colour rather than the fruit inside.

Why grapefruit goes bad

Pitted, reddish-brown peel

Too coldStill good

What you'll seeSunken pits and reddish-brown patches on the peel after weeks in the fridge.

Why it happens

Grapefruit gets chilling injury in cold storage: pitting, reddish-brown discoloration, off-flavors and more decay. How easily depends on the variety and the season. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the fruit is firm, smells fresh and has no mold. Toss it if the flesh is watery or tastes off.

Prevent it next time
  • Use refrigerated grapefruit within two to three weeks
  • For up to a week, the counter is fine
Use it up
  • Juice it
  • Broil the halves with a little sugar

Soft spot with green or blue mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft, watery patch that grows white fuzz and then green or blue powder, or a sour-smelling soft rot.

Why it happens

Green mold, blue mold and sour rot are among the main diseases of grapefruit after harvest. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss a moldy grapefruit. Citrus is soft, juicy fruit, so mold can spread below the surface. Check the fruit that was touching it.

Prevent it next time
  • Remove any soft grapefruit as soon as you see it
  • Keep the fruit dry

Soft, shriveled peel

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe peel wrinkles and softens, and the fruit feels light for its size.

Why it happens

Grapefruit loses water through the peel and keeps best at 90 to 95 percent humidity. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if there's no mold and it smells fresh. There is less juice.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep grapefruit in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Juice it

What went wrong?

Grapefruit and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Grapefruit gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Grapefruit is somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing it away from ripening fruit helps.

The part people throw away

About 49% of grapefruit by weight is usually trimmed off as peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Candy the peels in simple syrup
  • Soak peels in vinegar for 2 weeks for a citrus cleaner
