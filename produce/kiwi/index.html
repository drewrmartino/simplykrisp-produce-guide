How to store kiwis, and why they go bad

Kiwi is extremely sensitive to ethylene. Ripe kiwi goes from soft to mushy quickly, especially near other ripening fruit.

Where to keep them
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long they last
1-4 weeks (refrigerated when ripe)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas and pears
What are you seeing?
Mushy, overly soft fruitDark, soft bruisesMold or a sour, fermented smell

How to store kiwis

  • Store unripe kiwi at room temperature until soft
  • Once ripe, move to the refrigerator
  • Keep away from ethylene-producing fruits until ready to ripen
  • Place near apples or bananas to speed ripening

Picking good kiwis: A ripe kiwi yields to gentle pressure, similar to a ripe pear.

Why kiwis go bad

Mushy, overly soft fruit

Ethylene gasCook it soon

What you'll seeThe fruit gives with almost no pressure, and the flesh is very soft and translucent.

Why it happens

Kiwi softens in response to tiny amounts of ethylene. UC Davis reports that as little as 5 to 10 parts per billion is enough. Ripe kiwi also gives off plenty of its own. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if it smells fresh and there's no mold. Toss it if it smells fermented.

Prevent it next time
  • Once ripe, keep kiwi in the fridge, away from apples, bananas and pears
  • Ripen only as many as you'll eat in a few days
Use it up
  • Blend it into a smoothie
  • Mash it over yogurt or oatmeal
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp makes a real difference here. Ripe kiwi turns mushy fast around ethylene. This slows the softening. Expect about 2–4 extra days once it is ripe and in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Dark, soft bruises

BruisingTrim and use

What you'll seeA dark, wet-looking patch in the flesh under a soft spot on the skin.

Why it happens

Ripe kiwi bruises easily. Wounds are also where gray mold gets in. 12

Is it still OK?

Cut away the bruised part and eat the rest today.

Prevent it next time
  • Store ripe kiwi in a single layer
  • Don't put heavier fruit on top of it
Use it up
  • Scoop the good flesh into a smoothie

Mold or a sour, fermented smell

MoldToss it

What you'll seeGray fuzz, usually at the stem end or a wound, or a fruit that smells sour or boozy.

Why it happens

Gray mold is the main kiwi disease after harvest. It can invade the fruit directly or through wounds. 13

Is it still OK?

Toss it. Kiwi is soft fruit, so mold can't be cut away.

Prevent it next time
  • Eat ripe kiwi within a few days
  • Remove any damaged fruit from the bag

What went wrong?

Kiwis and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Kiwis give off only a little ethylene, so they are fine next to most other produce.
  • Kiwis are very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast they age.

Keep them apart from apples, bananas and pears.

The part people throw away

About 24% of kiwis by weight is usually trimmed off as skins (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • The skin is edible: blend whole kiwi into smoothies
  • Slice thin, skin on, for a fiber boost
