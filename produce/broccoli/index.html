How to store broccoli, and why it goes bad

Broccoli is one of the most ethylene-sensitive vegetables you can buy. Yellow florets are the classic sign. It can also dry out or rot, especially at the cut stem.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
3-5 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas, peaches, tomatoes and cantaloupe
What are you seeing?
Florets turning yellowRubbery, bendy stemsDark, soft or fuzzy spots

How to store broccoli

  • Store unwashed in a loose plastic bag in the crisper
  • Do not seal tightly — broccoli needs air circulation
  • Wrap stems in a damp paper towel
  • Use within a few days for best nutrition and flavor

Picking good broccoli: Tight, dark green florets with firm stems. Avoid any yellowing.

Why broccoli goes bad

Florets turning yellow

Ethylene gasCook it soon

What you'll seeThe tiny buds on the florets turn from dark green to yellow, starting at the edges of the head.

Why it happens

Ethylene speeds up floret yellowing. UC Davis reports that 2 parts per million of ethylene at 50°F cuts broccoli's shelf life in half. Apples, bananas, peaches and tomatoes nearby are the usual source. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat but more bitter. Roasting or soup hides it best.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep broccoli away from apples, bananas, peaches and tomatoes
  • Store it cold, unwashed, in a loose bag in the crisper
Use it up
  • Roast it hot with lemon and parmesan
  • Blend it into broccoli soup
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp makes a real difference here. Keeps the florets green instead of yellowing. Expect about 2–4 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Rubbery, bendy stems

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe stalk bends instead of snapping, and the florets feel soft.

Why it happens

Broccoli keeps losing water after harvest. A tightly sealed bag isn't the answer, because it traps moisture and invites rot. A damp wrap on the stem works better. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. Trim the stem end and stand it in cold water for an hour to firm it up.

Prevent it next time
  • Wrap the stem end in a damp paper towel
  • Use a loose bag, not a sealed one
Use it up
  • Peel the stalk and slice it into stir-fry coins

Dark, soft or fuzzy spots

Bacterial rotTrim and use

What you'll seeBrown or black soft spots, a slimy patch, or gray or black fuzz on the florets or cut stem.

Why it happens

Bacterial soft rot, gray mold and black mold are the main causes of broccoli decay. They usually start where the head was damaged or got wet. 12

Is it still OK?

A small spot on the thick stalk can be cut out with an inch to spare around it. If the florets are moldy or slimy, toss the head.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't wash it until you're ready to cook
  • Use it within a few days of buying it

What went wrong?

Broccoli and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Broccoli gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Broccoli is very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast it ages.

Keep it apart from apples, bananas, peaches, tomatoes and cantaloupe.

The part people throw away

About 39% of broccoli by weight is usually trimmed off as stalks and leaves (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Peel stalks and slice into coins for stir-fry, or shred for slaw
  • Cook the leaves like kale
