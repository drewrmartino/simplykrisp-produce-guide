How to store parsley, and why it goes bad

Parsley is one of the longer-lasting herbs, a week or two. It yellows around ethylene and wilts when it dries out.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
1-2 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas and tomatoes
What are you seeing?
Leaves turning yellowWilted, limp sprigsSlimy or moldy leaves

How to store parsley

  • Trim the stems and stand the bunch in a jar with an inch of water
  • Cover loosely with a bag and keep the jar on a fridge shelf
  • Change the water every couple of days
  • Or wrap it in a barely damp paper towel inside a bag in the crisper drawer

Picking good parsley: Deep green, springy leaves with firm stems. Skip bunches with yellow or wilted leaves.

Does a Krisp help? In a jar on the shelf, parsley is out of the Krisp's reach. Bagged in the high-humidity drawer instead, the Krisp helps keep it from yellowing.

Why parsley goes bad

Leaves turning yellow

Ethylene gasTrim and use

What you'll seeLeaves fade from deep green to yellow.

Why it happens

Parsley gives off almost no ethylene but is very sensitive to it. USDA's storage handbook reports that as little as 0.4 parts per million speeds up yellowing once parsley is warmer than 32°F. 12

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. Pick off the yellow leaves and use the rest soon.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the jar on a different shelf from apples, pears and tomatoes
  • Keep it cold
Use it up
  • Blend it into chimichurri or pesto
  • Add the stems to stock

Wilted, limp sprigs

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeStems and leaves droop but are still green.

Why it happens

USDA's storage handbook marks the end of parsley's storage life as the point where it wilts, after losing about a fifth of its weight in water. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. Trim the stems and stand them in cold water for 30 minutes.

Prevent it next time
  • Stand the stems in water and cover the bunch loosely
  • Or wrap it in a barely damp paper towel inside a bag
Use it up
  • Chop and freeze it for cooking

Slimy or moldy leaves

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeLeaves turn dark and slimy, or grow gray fuzz.

Why it happens

Soft-rot bacteria and gray mold both attack stored parsley. Wet leaves in a closed bag give them what they need. 12

Is it still OK?

Pick out and toss the slimy or moldy sprigs. If the whole bunch smells off, toss it.

Prevent it next time
  • Do not wash parsley until you use it
  • Change the water in the jar every couple of days

What went wrong?

Parsley and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Parsley gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Parsley is very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast it ages.

Keep it apart from apples, bananas and tomatoes.

The part people throw away

About 5% of parsley by weight is usually trimmed off as tough stems (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Add stems to stock or a pot of beans
  • Blend tender stems into chimichurri or pesto
