How to store arugula, and why it goes bad

Arugula is one of the shortest-lived greens, a few days at home. It yellows, and damp leaves turn slimy.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
3-5 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas and pears
What are you seeing?
Leaves turning yellowSlimy, dark leavesLimp, wilted leaves

How to store arugula

  • Keep it in its container or a bag with a dry paper towel to soak up moisture
  • Store it in the crisper drawer on the humid setting
  • Do not wash it until you use it
  • Pick out yellow or slimy leaves so they do not spoil the rest

Picking good arugula: Dry, bright green leaves with no yellow, dark or slimy pieces.

Why arugula goes bad

Leaves turning yellow

Ethylene gasTrim and use

What you'll seeLeaves fade from green to yellow.

Why it happens

USDA's storage handbook says salad greens like arugula give off very little ethylene but are highly sensitive to it, and the result is yellow leaves. Even stored well, arugula lasts 7 to 10 days from harvest. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. Pick out the yellow leaves and use the rest soon.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep arugula away from apples, pears and bananas
  • Keep it cold and use it within a few days
Use it up
  • Wilt it into pasta or onto pizza
  • Blend it into pesto
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp helps a little here. Slows the leaves yellowing. Expect about 1–2 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Slimy, dark leaves

Trapped moistureToss it

What you'll seeLeaves turn dark, wet and slimy, usually at the bottom of the container.

Why it happens

Small, tender leaves break down fast when moisture collects in a closed container. USDA's storage handbook names bacterial soft rot as the most common decay in leafy greens. 1

Is it still OK?

Toss slimy or bad-smelling leaves. If only a few are affected, the dry, clean ones are fine after a good rinse.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep a dry paper towel in the container and change it when it gets damp
  • Do not wash arugula until you use it

Limp, wilted leaves

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeLeaves droop and feel soft instead of crisp.

Why it happens

Arugula leaves lose water quickly once they are out of the cold or left uncovered. Commercial storage keeps them at 95 percent humidity or higher, far above open fridge air. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if there is no slime or bad smell. Soak the leaves in cold water for 10 minutes to firm them back up.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep it in a bag in the crisper drawer
  • Get it into the fridge soon after you buy it
Use it up
  • Sauté it with garlic
  • Add it to soup

What went wrong?

Arugula and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Arugula gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Arugula is very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast it ages.

Keep it apart from apples, bananas and pears.
