How to store cantaloupe, and why it goes bad

A whole cantaloupe ripens on the counter and gives off a lot of ethylene as it does. Most losses come from letting it go too far, from rot on the rind, or from cut melon sitting too long.

Where to keep it
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long it lasts
5-7 days ripe (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 40°F (4°C)
Don't store next to
Leafy greens, broccoli and cucumbers
What are you seeing?
Soft, mushy fleshMold or sunken soft spots on the rindCut melon turned slimy or sour

How to store cantaloupe

  • Store whole melon on the counter until ripe
  • Once ripe, refrigerate immediately
  • Cut melon should be wrapped and stored in the fridge for up to 3 days
  • Keep away from ethylene-sensitive produce

Picking good cantaloupe: Ripe cantaloupe has a sweet fragrance at the stem end and yields slightly to pressure. The skin should have a golden undertone.

Does a Krisp help? Whole melons ripen on the counter and are too big for the drawer. Once cut, wrap it and eat it within a few days.

Why cantaloupe goes bad

Soft, mushy flesh

OverripeningCook it soon

What you'll seeThe melon gives easily all over, and the flesh is very soft and wet.

Why it happens

Cantaloupe keeps ripening after harvest and makes a lot of its own ethylene, so going overripe is the usual problem. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if it smells sweet. Use it today. If it smells sour or boozy, or the flesh is slimy, toss it.

Prevent it next time
  • Move it to the fridge as soon as the stem end smells sweet
  • Eat it within five to seven days of ripening
Use it up
  • Blend it into a smoothie or agua fresca
  • Freeze cubes for smoothies

Mold or sunken soft spots on the rind

MoldToss it

What you'll seeFuzzy white, green or black patches, or soft sunken spots, often at the stem end or where the melon sat.

Why it happens

Several fungi cause decay and surface spots on cantaloupe rind. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss a melon with mold or soft, sunken rot on the rind. FDA says to throw away produce that looks rotten, and a knife carries whatever is on the rind into the flesh.

Prevent it next time
  • Choose melons without soft spots, cracks or mold at the stem end
  • Keep the melon dry on the counter

Cut melon turned slimy or sour

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeCut pieces feel slick, look glassy or dull, and smell sour or fermented.

Why it happens

Once a melon is cut, the wet flesh is exposed and bacteria can grow on it. FDA notes that a knife can carry dirt and bacteria from the rind onto the flesh. 21

Is it still OK?

Toss it. Don't taste cut melon that is slimy or smells off.

Prevent it next time
  • Scrub the rind under running water with a clean brush before you cut it
  • Refrigerate cut melon right away in a covered container
  • Eat cut melon within about three days

What went wrong?

Cantaloupe and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Cantaloupe gives off a lot of ethylene as it ripens. Keep it away from greens, broccoli and other sensitive produce.
  • Cantaloupe is somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing it away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep it apart from leafy greens, broccoli and cucumbers.

The part people throw away

About 49% of cantaloupe by weight is usually trimmed off as rind and seeds (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Blend the seed cavity with water, strain and chill for agua fresca
  • Pickle the pale inner rind like watermelon rind
