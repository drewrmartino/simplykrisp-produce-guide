How to store pears, and why they go bad

Pears ripen from the inside out, so by the time one feels soft all over, the core is already past it. Bruising and mold are the other ways they go.

Where to keep them
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long they last
3-5 days ripe, 2-3 months unripe (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 30°F (-1°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas, kiwis, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, celery and lettuce
What are you seeing?
Mushy and brown at the coreBrown scuffs and bruisesSoft rot or fuzzy mold

How to store pears

  • Pears ripen from the inside out — check the neck near the stem
  • Ripen on the counter, then refrigerate
  • Unripe pears can last months in the fridge
  • Keep away from strong-smelling foods

Picking good pears: Press gently near the stem. If it yields, it's ripe. Pears ripen from the inside out, so don't wait until they feel soft all over.

Why pears go bad

Mushy and brown at the core

OverripeningCook it soon

What you'll seeThe neck is very soft, and the flesh around the core is brown, wet and mushy.

Why it happens

Pears ripen after harvest and make a lot of their own ethylene as they do, which keeps pushing them along. Past ripe, the flesh softens too far and breaks down. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if it smells sweet and there's no mold. Cut out the brown core and use the rest today.

Prevent it next time
  • Check the neck every day. When it gives to gentle pressure, the pear is ripe
  • Move ripe pears to the fridge, away from apples and bananas
Use it up
  • Simmer it into pear sauce or pear butter
  • Bake it into muffins or a crisp
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp helps a little here. Once ripe and chilled, slows it going soft and mealy. Expect about 1–2 extra days once it is ripe and in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Brown scuffs and bruises

BruisingTrim and use

What you'll seeBrown marks or bands on the skin, or soft brown flesh underneath.

Why it happens

Pears mark easily. Rubbing and vibration leave brown spots or bands on the skin, and an impact browns the flesh underneath. 12

Is it still OK?

Skin scuffs are cosmetic. Cut out any bruised flesh and eat the rest.

Prevent it next time
  • Carry and store pears in a single layer
  • Handle ripe pears as little as possible
Use it up
  • Slice the good parts into a salad or onto toast

Soft rot or fuzzy mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft, watery brown patch, sometimes with blue-green or gray fuzz.

Why it happens

Blue mold and gray mold are the main pear diseases after harvest. 134

Is it still OK?

Toss a pear that is moldy or rotting. Ripe pears are soft, juicy fruit, so mold can spread below the surface.

Prevent it next time
  • Remove damaged pears from the bowl or bag
  • Eat ripe pears within three to five days

What went wrong?

Pears and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Pears give off a lot of ethylene as they ripen. Keep them away from greens, broccoli and other sensitive produce.
  • Pears are very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast they age.

Keep them apart from apples, bananas, kiwis, Brussels sprouts, cauliflower, celery and lettuce.

The part people throw away

About 10% of pears by weight is usually trimmed off as cores and peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Simmer cores and peels for pear syrup
  • Add cores to apple-scrap vinegar
