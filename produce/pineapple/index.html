How to store pineapple, and why it goes bad

Pineapple doesn't get sweeter after it's picked, so there is nothing to wait for. Left too long it ferments, and too long in a cold fridge browns the core.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
3-5 days (counter), 5-7 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 50°F (10°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular

How to store pineapple

  • Store whole in the refrigerator for up to 5 days
  • Pineapples do not ripen further after picking — refrigerate right away
  • Once cut, store in an airtight container in the fridge for 3-4 days
  • Store upside down briefly to distribute sweetness before cutting

Picking good pineapple: A ripe pineapple has a sweet fragrance at the base, golden skin, and leaves pull out easily.

Does a Krisp help? Pineapple does not ripen further after harvest, so there is nothing for an absorber to slow down.

Why pineapple goes bad

Soft, fizzy flesh that smells like alcohol

AgeToss it

What you'll seeBright yellow, very soft flesh with gas pockets, and a smell of alcohol or vinegar.

Why it happens

Yeasts ferment the sugar in overripe pineapple. UC Davis describes soft, bright-yellow flesh broken up by large gas cavities. The yeast gets in through wounds. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it if it smells like alcohol or vinegar, fizzes, or has soft, leaking patches.

Prevent it next time
  • Eat pineapple within a few days of buying it
  • Choose one without soft spots, leaks or a fermented smell at the base

Brown, watery spots near the core

Too coldTrim and use

What you'll seeWater-soaked brown spots in and around the core, sometimes spreading until the center is brown.

Why it happens

Pineapple is damaged below 45°F, and a week or more is enough. Brown, water-soaked spots form in the core. Ripe fruit handles cold better than unripe fruit. 12

Is it still OK?

Cut away the brown, watery flesh. The rest is fine if it looks normal and smells sweet. Toss it if it smells fermented.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep a whole pineapple in the fridge for no more than about five days
  • Or keep it on the counter and eat it within a few days
Use it up
  • Grill or roast the good pieces
  • Freeze chunks for smoothies

Dark, soft patches on the shell

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA slightly darker patch of shell that gives way under light pressure, with soft, watery flesh underneath.

Why it happens

This is black rot, a fungal disease. The flesh under the patch softens, and the skin over it breaks easily. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it. FDA says to throw away produce that looks rotten.

Prevent it next time
  • Check the base and sides for soft spots before buying
  • Don't let a whole pineapple sit for more than a few days

What went wrong?

Pineapple and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Pineapple gives off only a little ethylene, so it is fine next to most other produce.
  • Pineapple is barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 49% of pineapple by weight is usually trimmed off as core, rind and crown (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Ferment the rind into tepache (rind, brown sugar, water, 2–3 days)
  • Slice the core thin for smoothies or the grill
  • Root the crown in water, then plant it
