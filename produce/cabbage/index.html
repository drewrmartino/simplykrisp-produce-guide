How to store cabbage, and why it goes bad

Cabbage is one of the longest-keeping vegetables, a month or two in the fridge. The outer leaves take the damage: they yellow around ethylene, speckle with age, and brown where the head was cut.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
1-2 months (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, avocados and peaches
What are you seeing?
Yellow, loose outer leavesSmall black specks on the leavesGray or brown cut edgeSlimy or moldy outer leaves

How to store cabbage

  • Store whole in the crisper drawer
  • Do not cut until ready to use
  • Wrap cut cabbage tightly in plastic wrap
  • Outer leaves may wilt but protect the inner head

Picking good cabbage: Choose heads that feel heavy and compact with crisp, tightly packed leaves.

Why cabbage goes bad

Yellow, loose outer leaves

Ethylene gasTrim and use

What you'll seeThe outer leaves turn yellow and separate from the head.

Why it happens

Ethylene makes cabbage leaves yellow and detach. Cabbage makes very little of it, so the source is usually fruit stored nearby. 1

Is it still OK?

Peel off the yellow leaves. The head underneath is fine if it's firm and crisp.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep cabbage away from apples, avocados and peaches
  • Store it whole in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Shred it for slaw
  • Sauté or braise it
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp makes a real difference here. Slows outer leaves yellowing and loosening from the head. Expect about 2–4 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Small black specks on the leaves

AgeStill good

What you'll seeTiny dark specks on the ribs and veins of the leaves, like ground pepper.

Why it happens

This is pepper spot, also called black leaf speck. It's a storage disorder, not mold. It's tied to cold weather in the field and late harvest, and it develops further when cabbage goes from cold storage to warmer temperatures. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. The specks are cosmetic. Peel off the outer leaves if they bother you.

Prevent it next time
  • Choose heads without specks on the outer leaves
  • Keep cabbage in the fridge rather than letting it sit out
Use it up
  • Use it cooked, where the specks disappear

Gray or brown cut edge

BruisingTrim and use

What you'll seeThe cut face of a half head turns gray-brown or black.

Why it happens

Cut and broken cabbage tissue browns. UC Davis notes that broken ribs brown and decay more easily too. 12

Is it still OK?

Shave off the discolored layer. The cabbage underneath is fine if it's crisp and smells fresh.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't cut the head until you need it
  • Wrap the cut face tightly
  • Peel off whole leaves when you only need a little
Use it up
  • Shred the rest for slaw or a stir-fry

Slimy or moldy outer leaves

Bacterial rotTrim and use

What you'll seeDark, slimy, bad-smelling leaves, or gray fuzz on the outside of the head.

Why it happens

Bacterial soft rot breaks leaves down into slime. Gray mold and other fungi also attack stored cabbage. 13

Is it still OK?

Cabbage is firm, so USDA says a small mold spot can be cut off with at least an inch around and below it. Peel away the affected outer leaves as well. If the slime or smell reaches into the head, toss it.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the head dry and unwashed until you use it
  • Check stored cabbage every week or two and peel off any leaves that are going
Use it up
  • Cook what is left the same day

What went wrong?

Cabbage and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Cabbage gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Cabbage is very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast it ages.

Keep it apart from apples, avocados and peaches.

The part people throw away

About 20% of cabbage by weight is usually trimmed off as outer leaves and core (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Use big outer leaves as wraps or for cabbage rolls
  • Slice the core thin into slaw or stir-fry
