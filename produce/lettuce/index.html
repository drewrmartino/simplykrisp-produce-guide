How to store lettuce, and why it goes bad

Romaine and leaf lettuce usually fail in one of three ways: brown specks from ethylene, limp leaves from drying out, or slime from bacteria. Only the last one means it's done.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
7-10 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas, pears, peaches and tomatoes
What are you seeing?
Brown or rusty specks on the ribsLimp, floppy leavesDark, wet, slimy leaves

How to store lettuce

  • Store unwashed in a bag lined with paper towels
  • Keep in the crisper drawer on the humid setting
  • Remove damaged outer leaves to prevent spreading decay
  • Never store near apples, pears, or bananas

Picking good lettuce: Crisp, vibrant leaves with no browning or slimy spots.

Why lettuce goes bad

Brown or rusty specks on the ribs

Ethylene gasStill good

What you'll seeSmall tan-to-brown spots, mostly along the pale midribs of romaine and leaf lettuce.

Why it happens

This is russet spotting. Romaine reacts to as little as 1 part per million of ethylene, the gas apples, pears, bananas and tomatoes give off. A shared crisper drawer can easily reach that. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. The spots can taste a little bitter, so trim the worst ribs if they bother you.

Prevent it next time
  • Give lettuce its own drawer, away from apples, pears, bananas and tomatoes
  • Store it unwashed in a bag lined with paper towels
Use it up
  • Shred it into tacos, wraps or fried rice, where the spots won't show
  • Halve romaine hearts and char them in a hot pan or on the grill
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp makes a real difference here. Slows russet spotting, the brown specks on romaine and leaf lettuce. Expect about 2–4 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Limp, floppy leaves

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeLeaves bend instead of snapping, and the edges look thin or papery.

Why it happens

Lettuce has a lot of leaf surface and loses water quickly. Dry fridge air, or a bag left open, pulls moisture out of it. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. Limp lettuce is only thirsty, and it usually firms back up.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep it in the crisper drawer on the humid setting
  • Wrap it loosely in a damp (not wet) paper towel inside a bag
Use it up
  • Soak the leaves in ice water for 15 to 30 minutes, then spin them dry
  • Wilt limp outer leaves into soup or a stir-fry

Dark, wet, slimy leaves

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeTranslucent or dark green patches that feel slick, often with a sour smell.

Why it happens

Soft-rot bacteria take hold in leaves that are damaged, wet or frozen. Lettuce pushed against the back wall of the fridge can freeze, and those leaves turn slimy fast once they thaw. Rot spreads faster when it's warm and humid. 1

Is it still OK?

Toss the slimy leaves and the ones touching them. If the bag smells off or most of it is slimy, toss the whole thing.

Prevent it next time
  • Dry leaves well before storing, and keep a paper towel in the bag to catch condensation
  • Keep lettuce away from the back wall of the fridge, where it can freeze
  • Pull off damaged outer leaves before they spread decay

What went wrong?

Lettuce and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Lettuce gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Lettuce is very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast it ages.

Keep it apart from apples, bananas, pears, peaches and tomatoes.

The part people throw away

About 6% of lettuce by weight is usually trimmed off as core and outer leaves (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Set a romaine base in water to regrow a few leaves
  • Wilt outer leaves into soup or stir-fry
