Raspberries: what went wrong, and what to do

Raspberries are among the most perishable fruits you can buy, lasting only two to five days even at 32°F. Mold is almost always what ends them.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
2-3 days (refrigerated)
Keep it away from
Nothing in particular

1

Gray, fuzzy mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeGray fuzz on a few berries, spreading through the container.

Why it happens

Diseases cause most raspberry losses, and gray mold (Botrytis) is the main one. It keeps growing slowly even at 32°F. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss moldy berries and any soft or wet ones touching them. You can't cut mold out of soft fruit.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them dry and unwashed until you eat them
  • Store them in a single layer on a paper towel, in the coldest part of the fridge
  • Plan to eat them within two days
2

Leaking, collapsing berries

BruisingCook it soon

What you'll seeBerries lose their shape, leak juice and stick to each other, with no mold.

Why it happens

Raspberries are hollow and fragile, and they soften quickly after harvest. Crushing speeds it up. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if there's no mold. Use them today.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't stack containers
  • Handle them as little as possible
Use it up
  • Cook them into a quick sauce
  • Freeze them for smoothies
3

Berries turning dark purple-red

Ethylene gasStill good

What you'll seeRed raspberries darken toward purple.

Why it happens

Ethylene can darken red raspberries to purple-red, and it can also speed up gray mold. Raspberries are too short-lived for a Krisp to make a real difference, so the fix is to keep them away from fruit and eat them fast. 3

Is it still OK?

Still good if there's no mold. The color change is cosmetic.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep raspberries away from apples, bananas and other ripening fruit

How to store raspberries

  • Do not wash until ready to eat — moisture causes mold rapidly
  • Store in a single layer on a paper towel-lined container
  • Remove any moldy berries immediately to prevent spreading
  • Keep in the coldest part of the refrigerator

Does a Krisp help? Grey mould, not ethylene, is what ends a raspberry.

Full storage details are in the Produce Storage Guide.

The part people throw away

About 4% of raspberries by weight is usually trimmed off as soft berries (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Cook soft (not moldy) berries into a quick sauce
  • Freeze for smoothies
