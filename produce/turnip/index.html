How to store turnips, and why they go bad

Turnips keep for weeks in the cold. They shrivel as they dry out, and warm storage brings on soft rot.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
2-3 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Soft, shriveled turnipsSoft, wet or moldy spotsYellow, wilted greens

How to store turnips

  • Cut off the greens so they stop pulling moisture out of the root
  • Store the unwashed roots in a plastic bag in the crisper drawer
  • Use the greens within 2 to 3 days. Cook them like mustard greens
  • Peel large turnips before cooking. Small ones only need a scrub

Picking good turnips: Small, heavy and firm with smooth skin. Large turnips can be woody and sharp-tasting.

Does a Krisp help? Turnips barely react to ethylene. They go soft from drying out, so a bag in the drawer does more than a Krisp.

Why turnips go bad

Soft, shriveled turnips

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe roots feel spongy or look wrinkled.

Why it happens

Turnips shrivel as they lose water. USDA's storage handbook says to keep them cold and at high humidity to slow it, and that storage above 41°F speeds up weight loss. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if there are no dark, soft spots and they smell fresh.

Prevent it next time
  • Cut off the greens
  • Keep the roots in a bag in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Roast them
  • Mash them with potatoes

Soft, wet or moldy spots

Bacterial rotTrim and use

What you'll seeSoft, watery, sunken patches, sometimes with gray fuzz.

Why it happens

USDA's storage handbook says storing turnips warmer than 41°F speeds up soft rot. Gray mold and watery soft rot are also listed. 12

Is it still OK?

Turnips are firm, so USDA says a small mold spot can be cut off with at least an inch around and below it. Toss one that is slimy or soft all the way through.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them cold
  • Keep them dry in their bag

Yellow, wilted greens

AgeTrim and use

What you'll seeThe leafy tops turn limp and yellow while the roots are still firm.

Why it happens

The tops age much faster than the roots. USDA's storage handbook calls turnip greens particularly perishable. 1

Is it still OK?

Cut off and discard yellow or slimy tops. The roots are fine if they are firm.

Prevent it next time
  • Cut the greens off when you get home and store them separately
  • Use the greens within 2 to 3 days
Use it up
  • Cook fresh greens like mustard greens

What went wrong?

Turnips and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Turnips give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Turnips are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 19% of turnips by weight is usually trimmed off as peels and greens (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Cook the greens like mustard greens
  • Small turnips only need a scrub, not peeling
