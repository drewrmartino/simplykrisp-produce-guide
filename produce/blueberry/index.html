How to store blueberries, and why they go bad

Blueberries last longer than most berries, a week or two in the fridge. Mold is what usually ends them, followed by shriveling.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
1-2 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular

How to store blueberries

  • Do not wash until ready to eat — moisture promotes mold
  • Store in a single layer on a paper towel-lined container
  • Keep in the coldest part of the refrigerator
  • Remove any moldy berries immediately

Picking good blueberries: Look for firm, plump berries with a uniform deep blue color and a silvery frost coating.

Does a Krisp help? Berries barely react to ethylene. Mold is what ends them, so keep them dry and rinse just before eating.

Why blueberries go bad

Gray or white fuzzy mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeFuzz on a few berries, often where they touch each other or at the stem scar.

Why it happens

Gray mold and Rhizopus rot are the main diseases of blueberries after harvest. Moisture on the berries helps both along. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss moldy berries and any soft or wet ones touching them. You can't cut mold out of soft fruit.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep berries dry and unwashed until you eat them
  • Pick through the container when you get home and remove soft or damaged berries
  • Store them in the coldest part of the fridge

Wrinkled, shriveled berries

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeBerries lose their shine, wrinkle and feel soft.

Why it happens

Water loss makes blueberries shrivel and lose their gloss. It happens faster in an open container or in dry fridge air. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no mold. The texture is better cooked or blended.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them covered in the fridge
  • Eat them within a week or two
Use it up
  • Bake them into muffins or pancakes
  • Freeze them for smoothies

Soft, leaking berries

AgeCook it soon

What you'll seeBerries turn mushy and leak juice that stains the container, with no fuzz.

Why it happens

UC Davis calls these leakers: berries that break down inside as they age and leak juice. 1

Is it still OK?

Soft berries with no mold and no sour smell are fine cooked or frozen today. Toss any that smell fermented or have fuzz.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't stack anything on the container
  • Line the container with a paper towel to catch juice
Use it up
  • Simmer them into a quick compote for yogurt or oatmeal
  • Freeze them for smoothies

What went wrong?

Blueberries and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Blueberries give off only a little ethylene, so they are fine next to most other produce.
  • Blueberries are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 5% of blueberries by weight is usually trimmed off as soft berries and stems (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Cook soft (not moldy) berries into a quick compote for oatmeal or yogurt
  • Freeze soft berries for smoothies
