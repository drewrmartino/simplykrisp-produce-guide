Bananas: what went wrong, and what to do

Most banana problems look worse than they are. A dark peel usually means cold or ripeness, not spoilage.

Where to keep it
Counter
How long it lasts

1

Peel turns brown or black in the fridge

Too coldStill good

What you'll seeThe peel goes dull, gray or dark brown after time in the fridge, while the fruit inside stays pale.

Why it happens

Bananas are tropical and are damaged below about 56°F. The peel discolors and unripe bananas may stop ripening. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the banana was ripe when it went in. The peel looks bad, but the fruit is fine.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep bananas on the counter
  • Refrigerate them only once they're fully ripe
2

Covered in brown spots

OverripeningStill good

What you'll seeLots of brown speckles, with soft, sweet flesh.

Why it happens

Bananas keep ripening on the counter, driven by their own ethylene, and they ripen faster when it's warm. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good, and at their sweetest for baking.

Prevent it next time
  • Separate bananas from the bunch
  • Keep them away from other fruit
Use it up
  • Peel and freeze them for smoothies or banana bread
  • Mash them into pancakes or oatmeal
3

Brown, mushy patches inside

BruisingTrim and use

What you'll seeThe peel looks fine, but the flesh has brown, soft spots.

Why it happens

A drop or bump can brown the flesh without breaking the peel. 1

Is it still OK?

Cut out the bruised part and eat the rest.

Prevent it next time
  • Hang bananas or set them down gently
Use it up
  • Use bruised bananas in baking or smoothies
4

Mold on the stem or crown

MoldTrim and use

What you'll seeWhite, gray or black fuzz where the bananas join together.

Why it happens

Crown rot, a fungal infection of the cut stem where the bunch was separated, is one of the main ways bananas spoil. 1

Is it still OK?

If the mold is only on the stem and the fruit is firm and smells normal, cut off the stem end and eat it. If the fruit is leaking, smells fermented or has mold on it, toss it.

Prevent it next time
  • Check the crown when buying
  • Use bananas with a moldy crown first

How to store bananas

  • Keep on the counter at room temperature — never refrigerate unripe bananas
  • Wrap the stem in plastic wrap to slow ethylene release and extend freshness
  • Separate from the bunch to slow ripening
  • Only refrigerate if very ripe and you need to buy a couple extra days — the peel will brown but the fruit stays good

Does a Krisp help? Bananas belong on the counter, and a Krisp works inside the closed crisper drawer, so it can't reach them. Keep bananas away from your other produce instead. They give off ethylene too.

Full storage details are in the Produce Storage Guide.

The part people throw away

About 36% of bananas by weight is usually trimmed off as peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Blend one washed peel into banana bread or a smoothie
  • Shred peels and sauté with spices as a "pulled" taco filling
