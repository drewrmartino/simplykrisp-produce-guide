How to store green beans, and why they go bad

Green beans are fussy: too cold and they spot, too dry and they go limp, and ethylene yellows them. They are best within five to seven days.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
5-7 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 45°F (7°C)
Don't store next to
Apples and bananas
What are you seeing?
Yellowing and browningRusty brown spotsLimp, shriveled beansSlimy or moldy clumps

How to store green beans

  • Store unwashed in a breathable bag in the crisper
  • Do not snap or trim until ready to cook
  • Keep away from ethylene-producing fruits
  • Blanch and freeze for long-term storage

Picking good green beans: Firm, bright green, and snap cleanly when bent. Avoid limp or spotted beans.

Why green beans go bad

Yellowing and browning

Ethylene gasCook it soon

What you'll seeThe pods lose their bright green and turn yellowish or brown.

Why it happens

Ethylene makes green beans lose their green color and brown faster. UC Davis reports that more than 0.1 part per million cuts their shelf life at 41°F by 30 to 50 percent. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if they aren't slimy or moldy. They are past their best raw.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them away from apples, bananas and other ripening fruit
  • Store them unwashed in a breathable bag in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Braise them with tomatoes and garlic
  • Add them to soup or a stir-fry
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp helps a little here. Slows yellowing and browning. Expect about 2–3 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Rusty brown spots

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeSmall rusty-brown spots on the pods, sometimes with a dull, cloudy look.

Why it happens

Green beans keep best at 41 to 45°F, a little warmer than most fridges. Rusty-brown spots are the most common sign of cold damage. Below 41°F for more than five or six days, the pods can also turn dull and pit. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if the beans are firm and not slimy. Cook them soon, because cold-damaged beans decay faster.

Prevent it next time
  • Use green beans within five to seven days
  • Keep them in the crisper drawer, not against the cold back wall
Use it up
  • Blanch and freeze them
  • Roast or sauté them

Limp, shriveled beans

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeThe beans bend instead of snapping, and the skin wrinkles.

Why it happens

Green beans lose water quickly. UC Davis notes that about 5 percent weight loss is enough to make them shrivel and go limp. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no slime or mold.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them in a bag in the crisper drawer
  • Don't snap or trim them until you cook them
Use it up
  • Soak them in ice water for 30 minutes before cooking
  • Simmer them in soup or a stew

Slimy or moldy clumps

MoldToss it

What you'll seeWet, slimy patches, or white cottony fuzz holding several beans together.

Why it happens

Decay starts on broken or damaged beans and spreads to the ones touching them, forming what UC Davis calls nests. Moisture on the surface makes it worse. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss slimy or moldy beans and the ones stuck to them. Beans are soft and moist, so mold can't be cut away. If the rest are dry, firm and smell fresh, wash and cook them today.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep beans dry and unwashed until you cook them
  • Pick out broken or damaged beans when you get home

What went wrong?

Green beans and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Green beans give off only a little ethylene, so they are fine next to most other produce.
  • Green beans are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep them apart from apples and bananas.

The part people throw away

About 12% of green beans by weight is usually trimmed off as ends (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Add ends to a freezer stock bag
  • Compost the rest
