Why it goes bad

Spinach: what went wrong, and what to do

Spinach goes yellow from ethylene, wilts as it dries out, and turns slimy when bacteria get into crushed or wet leaves. Yellow and wilted spinach is still good for cooking.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
5-7 days (refrigerated)
Keep it away from
Apples, Bananas, Tomatoes and Peaches
What are you seeing?

Leaves turning yellow

Ethylene gasCook it soon

What you'll seeLeaves fade from dark green to pale or yellow, starting at the edges or with the oldest leaves.

Why it happens

Spinach is highly sensitive to ethylene, and even low levels make the leaves yellow faster. Fruit in the same drawer is the usual source. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. Yellow leaves taste flatter, so they're better cooked than in a salad.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep spinach away from apples, bananas, tomatoes and peaches
  • Store it in a container lined with paper towels
Use it up
  • Wilt it into eggs, pasta or soup
  • Blend it into a smoothie or pesto
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp makes a real difference here. Slows the leaves yellowing. Expect about 2–3 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Wet, dark, slimy leaves

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeLeaves clump together, look dark and wet, and may smell sour.

Why it happens

Bacterial soft rot usually starts on crushed stems and damaged leaves. A packed clamshell bruises leaves, and condensation gives bacteria the moisture they need. Spinach also freezes just below 32°F, and frozen leaves rot quickly after they thaw. 1

Is it still OK?

Pick out and toss slimy leaves, plus any stuck to them. If the container smells off, toss it all.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't pack it down. Give leaves room in the container
  • Line the container with a paper towel and replace it if it gets wet
  • Keep spinach away from the back wall of the fridge

Wilted, droopy leaves

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeLeaves have gone soft and floppy but are still green and dry.

Why it happens

Spinach leaves are thin and lose water fast. An open bag in dry fridge air speeds that up. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good to cook, since spinach wilts in the pan anyway.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the container closed, with a paper towel inside
  • Store it in the high-humidity crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Sauté it with garlic and olive oil
  • Freeze it in a bag to drop into smoothies or soups

How to store spinach

  • Store in a container lined with paper towels
  • Do not wash until ready to use
  • Remove any slimy or wilted leaves
  • Keep away from ethylene-producing fruits

Full storage details are in the Produce Storage Guide.

The part people throw away

About 28% of spinach by weight is usually trimmed off as stems (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Sauté stems with garlic
  • Blend stems into pesto or a smoothie
