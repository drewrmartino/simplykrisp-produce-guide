Why it goes bad

Mushrooms: what went wrong, and what to do

Mushrooms don't care about ethylene. They fail from moisture, bruising and age, so how you package them matters most.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
7-10 days (refrigerated)
Keep it away from
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Slimy, sticky surfaceBrown spots or darkeningCaps opening, gills showingDry, wrinkled mushrooms

Slimy, sticky surface

Trapped moistureToss it

What you'll seeCaps feel slick or sticky, look darker, and may smell sour or fishy.

Why it happens

Plastic traps moisture, and wet mushrooms are an easy home for spoilage bacteria. Warm temperatures and long storage make it worse. 1

Is it still OK?

Toss mushrooms that are slimy, sticky or smell sour.

Prevent it next time
  • Store them in a paper bag, not plastic
  • Keep them dry, and clean them with a damp cloth just before cooking

Brown spots or darkening

BruisingStill good

What you'll seeCaps get brown patches or darken overall, but they're still dry and firm.

Why it happens

Mushrooms bruise easily, and handling makes them brown. UC Davis notes that most mushroom losses come from bruising, rough handling and age, not disease. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if they're dry and smell normal. Cook them.

Prevent it next time
  • Handle them gently and don't stack heavy things on top
Use it up
  • Chop them fine into duxelles for toast, eggs or pasta

Caps opening, gills showing

AgeCook it soon

What you'll seeThe edge of the cap pulls away from the stem and the dark gills show.

Why it happens

Mushrooms keep growing after harvest, so the caps open and the veil breaks over time. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat, with a stronger flavor. Cook them soon.

Prevent it next time
  • Use them within about a week
Use it up
  • Use them in soup, gravy or a mushroom sauce

Dry, wrinkled mushrooms

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeMushrooms shrink, wrinkle and feel leathery.

Why it happens

Mushrooms are mostly water. Left uncovered or in a paper bag for too long, they dry out. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if there's no slime or mold. They work well cooked.

Prevent it next time
  • Fold the paper bag closed
  • Use them within about a week
Use it up
  • Slice them into soups and sauces
  • Dry them the rest of the way in a low oven and grind them into mushroom powder

How to store mushrooms

  • Store in a paper bag — never in plastic (traps moisture)
  • Do not wash until ready to cook
  • Keep in the main section of the fridge, not the crisper
  • Clean with a damp cloth or brush instead of rinsing

Does a Krisp help? The one head-to-head trial of an ethylene absorber in mushroom packaging found no difference in shelf life.

Full storage details are in the Produce Storage Guide.

The part people throw away

About 3% of mushrooms by weight is usually trimmed off as stems and trimmings (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Chop stems fine into duxelles for toast, eggs or pasta
  • Add to a stock bag for a deep broth
