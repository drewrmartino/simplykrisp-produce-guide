Why it goes bad

Bell peppers: what went wrong, and what to do

Peppers barely react to ethylene. They wrinkle as they lose water, get pitted if they're too cold for too long, and eventually mold.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
1-2 weeks (refrigerated)
Keep it away from
Nothing in particular

Soft, wrinkled skin

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeThe skin wrinkles and the walls feel soft instead of crisp.

Why it happens

Peppers soften as they lose water, and red, yellow and orange peppers do it faster than green ones. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if there's no mold. Wrinkled peppers are great cooked.

Prevent it next time
  • Store them whole and unwashed in the crisper drawer
  • Use colored peppers before green ones
Use it up
  • Roast them and blend into a sauce or dip
  • Slice them into fajitas or a stir-fry

Pitting or watery patches

Too coldTrim and use

What you'll seeSmall sunken pits or water-soaked areas on the skin, sometimes with rot at the stem.

Why it happens

Peppers kept at 41°F can show cold damage after about two weeks. Green peppers are more sensitive than colored ones. 1

Is it still OK?

Cut away the pitted spots and use the rest soon. Toss it if it's soft and rotting.

Prevent it next time
  • Use peppers within one to two weeks
  • Keep them out of the coldest spot in the fridge
Use it up
  • Dice it into salsa, eggs or chili

Mold spot on the skin or stem

MoldTrim and use

What you'll seeGray or black fuzz, usually near the stem or on a damaged spot.

Why it happens

Gray mold grows well even at fridge temperatures, and it gets in through cuts and punctures. 12

Is it still OK?

Bell peppers are firm, so USDA says a small mold spot can be cut off with at least an inch around and below it. If there's mold inside the seed cavity or it covers more than a small spot, toss it.

Prevent it next time
  • Store cut peppers in an airtight container and use them within a few days

How to store bell peppers

  • Store whole in the crisper drawer
  • Do not wash until ready to use
  • Cut peppers should be stored in an airtight container
  • Green peppers last longer than red, yellow, or orange

Does a Krisp help? Low ethylene sensitivity — peppers soften from water loss, not from ethylene.

Full storage details are in the Produce Storage Guide.

The part people throw away

About 18% of bell peppers by weight is usually trimmed off as tops and cores (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Cut the flesh off around the stem and dice it into salsa or eggs
  • Add tops (minus most seeds) to a freezer stock bag
