How to store avocados, and why they go bad

Avocados have a short window. They go from ripe to overripe fast, brown inside if chilled too early, and turn rancid when rot sets in.

Where to keep them
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long they last
3-5 days ripe (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 38°F (3°C)
Don't store next to
Bananas, apples, peaches, asparagus and cabbage
What are you seeing?
Mushy and overripeBrown streaks or gray fleshBlack, stringy, sour or moldy

How to store avocados

  • Leave on the counter to ripen, then move to the refrigerator
  • To speed ripening, place in a paper bag with a banana
  • Once ripe, refrigerate to slow further ripening
  • Store cut avocado with the pit in, sprinkled with lemon juice, wrapped tightly

Picking good avocados: Gently squeeze — a ripe avocado yields to firm pressure without being mushy. The skin darkens when ripe.

Why avocados go bad

Mushy and overripe

OverripeningStill good

What you'll seeThe fruit gives too easily when pressed, and the flesh is very soft.

Why it happens

Avocados ripen after harvest, and their own ethylene speeds up sharply as they ripen. At room temperature they can go from ripe to overripe in a day or two. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the flesh is green and smells fresh.

Prevent it next time
  • Move ripe avocados into the fridge to slow them down
  • Keep unripe ones on the counter, away from bananas if you don't want them to speed up
Use it up
  • Make guacamole or avocado toast
  • Blend it into a smoothie or salad dressing
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp helps a little here. Once ripe and chilled, slows it going from ripe to overripe. Expect about 2–3 extra days once it is ripe and in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Brown streaks or gray flesh

Too coldTrim and use

What you'll seeBrown threads or gray patches in the flesh, or a fruit that never ripens properly.

Why it happens

Unripe avocados kept cold for too long get internal browning and may never ripen. Avocados should go in the fridge only once they're ripe. 1

Is it still OK?

Cut away the brown parts. The rest is fine if it smells and tastes normal.

Prevent it next time
  • Ripen avocados on the counter first, then refrigerate
Use it up
  • Mash the good part with lime and salt

Black, stringy, sour or moldy

MoldToss it

What you'll seeDark rot spreading from the stem or from black spots on the skin, with a sour or rancid smell.

Why it happens

Fungal rots such as anthracnose and stem-end rot spread into the flesh, browning it and giving it a rancid flavor. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it if it smells sour or has visible mold. Avocados are soft fruit, so cutting around mold isn't safe.

Prevent it next time
  • Eat ripe avocados within a few days
  • Check the stem end when buying. Green underneath is good, brown isn't

Avocados and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Avocados give off a lot of ethylene as they ripen. Keep them away from greens, broccoli and other sensitive produce.
  • Avocados are very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast they age.

Keep them apart from bananas, apples, peaches, asparagus and cabbage.

The part people throw away

About 26% of avocados by weight is usually trimmed off as pit and skin (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Sprout the pit in water as a houseplant
  • Simmer pits and skins for a natural pink fabric dye
