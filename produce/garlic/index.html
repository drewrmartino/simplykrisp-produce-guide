How to store garlic, and why it goes bad

Garlic keeps for months in a dry pantry. When it goes, it sprouts, dries out into shrunken cloves, or turns soft and moldy.

Where to keep it
Cool, dark pantry
How long it lasts
3-6 months (pantry)
Best temperature
About 60°F (16°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular

How to store garlic

  • Store in a cool, dark, dry place with good ventilation
  • Do not refrigerate whole bulbs — moisture causes sprouting
  • Keep in a mesh bag or open container
  • Once broken from the bulb, use cloves within 10 days

Picking good garlic: Firm bulbs with tight, papery skin. Avoid soft, sprouting, or shriveled cloves.

Does a Krisp help? Garlic is a dry pantry item at 65–70% humidity. Sprouting and damp are what get it, not ethylene.

Why garlic goes bad

Green shoots

AgeStill good

What you'll seeA green shoot grows from the top of the clove, or shows in the center when you cut it.

Why it happens

Sprouting means the garlic has come out of dormancy. It happens fastest between about 40 and 65°F, cooler than a kitchen and close to fridge temperature. 12

Is it still OK?

Still okay to eat, according to Utah State University Extension. The shoot means the garlic is older and can taste bitter, so pull it out if you like.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep whole bulbs in a dry, airy spot at room temperature, not in the fridge
  • Buy garlic in amounts you'll use within a few months
Use it up
  • Roast the cloves, which mellows the flavor

Shrunken, soft cloves

Drying outTrim and use

What you'll seeCloves feel light, soft or hollow under the papery skin.

Why it happens

Garlic slowly loses water. UC Davis notes that the bulbs end up soft, spongy and shriveled. 23

Is it still OK?

Use the cloves that are still firm. Toss any that are soft, brown or sticky.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep bulbs whole until you need them
  • Once a bulb is broken open, use the cloves within about 10 days
Use it up
  • Peel the firm cloves and freeze them

Soft, moldy cloves

MoldToss it

What you'll seeBlue-green or gray powder on the cloves, or cloves that are soft, spongy and crumbling.

Why it happens

This is Penicillium rot. A bulb can look normal outside while the cloves turn soft and spongy, then break down into a green or gray powdery mass. 23

Is it still OK?

Toss the bulb. Michigan State University Extension says to throw out garlic when it's soft or moldy.

Prevent it next time
  • Store garlic dry, with air moving around it
  • Don't keep it in a sealed bag or container

What went wrong?

Garlic and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Garlic gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Garlic is barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 13% of garlic by weight is usually trimmed off as skins and root ends (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Add skins to a stock bag for flavor (strain them out)
  • Compost the rest
