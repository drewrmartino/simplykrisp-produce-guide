How to store potatoes, and why they go bad

Potatoes keep for weeks in a cool, dark pantry. Light turns them green, time makes them sprout, and one rotting potato can spoil the bag.

Where to keep them
Cool, dark pantry
How long they last
2-5 weeks (pantry)
Best temperature
About 45°F (7°C)
Don't store next to
Onions and apples
What are you seeing?
SproutsGreen skinSoft, wet, smelly potatoDark spots under the skin

How to store potatoes

  • Store in a cool, dark, well-ventilated area (not the refrigerator)
  • Keep in a paper bag, basket, or open container
  • Never store with onions — they release gases that cause sprouting
  • Avoid exposure to light which causes greening

Picking good potatoes: Firm with no green spots, sprouts, or soft areas.

Does a Krisp help? The research genuinely disagrees here — some studies show ethylene breaking dormancy, others show it suppressing sprouts. Cool, dark and ventilated is what matters.

Why potatoes go bad

Sprouts

AgeTrim and use

What you'll seeWhite, pink or purple sprouts growing from the eyes.

Why it happens

Potatoes sprout once their dormancy ends, and a warm room brings it on sooner. 123

Is it still OK?

If the potato is still firm, cut out the sprouts and eyes and use it. University of Illinois Extension says a firm sprouted potato is fine once the sprouts are removed. If it's also shriveled or wrinkled, don't eat it.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep potatoes cool, dark and ventilated
  • Buy what you'll use within a few weeks
Use it up
  • Cut out the sprouts, peel it and cook it this week

Green skin

LightTrim and use

What you'll seeGreen patches on or just under the skin.

Why it happens

Light makes potatoes produce chlorophyll, which is the green you see. Bitter, toxic compounds called glycoalkaloids form along with it. Solanine is the best known. 321

Is it still OK?

Cut out small green areas. NDSU Extension says that removes most of the toxin. Peel away any green skin too. If the greening is extensive, the flesh under the skin is green, or the potato tastes bitter, throw it away.

Prevent it next time
  • Store potatoes in the dark, in a paper bag, a basket or a closed cupboard
  • Don't leave them out on the counter
Use it up
  • Peel and trim it, then cook it as usual

Soft, wet, smelly potato

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeA soft, wet or leaking potato with a strong bad smell.

Why it happens

Bacterial soft rot and several fungal rots break potatoes down. Bruised, cut or skinned potatoes are the most likely to decay. 34

Is it still OK?

Toss it. FDA says to throw away produce that looks rotten. Check the potatoes that were touching it and wipe out the bin.

Prevent it next time
  • Check the bag every week or so and remove any soft potato
  • Keep potatoes dry and unwashed until you use them

Dark spots under the skin

BruisingTrim and use

What you'll seeGray, blue-black or brown patches in the flesh when you peel or cut the potato.

Why it happens

This is bruising. Bruised potato tissue turns reddish, then blue-black, over one to three days. 34

Is it still OK?

Cut the dark spots out. The rest of the potato is fine if it's firm.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't drop the bag or toss potatoes into a bin
  • Store them where they will not get knocked around
Use it up
  • Trim it and cook it as usual

What went wrong?

Potatoes and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Potatoes give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Potatoes are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep them apart from onions and apples.

The part people throw away

About 25% of potatoes by weight is usually trimmed off as peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Toss peels with oil and salt and bake at 400°F until crisp
  • Skip any green spots
