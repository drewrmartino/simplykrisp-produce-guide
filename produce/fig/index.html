How to store figs, and why they go bad

Fresh figs last two or three days. They sour, mold and collapse quickly, so buy them close to when you'll eat them.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
2-3 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)

Nothing in particular

How to store figs

  • Store in a single layer on a paper towel-lined plate
  • Refrigerate immediately — figs are extremely perishable
  • Bring to room temperature before eating for best flavor
  • Do not stack or they will bruise and spoil quickly

Picking good figs: Slightly soft to the touch with a sweet fragrance. The skin may have small cracks near the base when perfectly ripe.

Does a Krisp help? Low sensitivity, and figs already give off a fair amount of ethylene themselves.

Why figs go bad

Sour or boozy smell, oozing

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeThe fig smells like alcohol or vinegar and may ooze liquid from the bottom.

Why it happens

This is souring. Yeasts and bacteria ferment the fruit, and it smells of alcohol or vinegar. It usually starts before harvest, carried in by insects such as vinegar flies. 1

Is it still OK?

Toss figs that smell sour or boozy, or that are oozing.

Prevent it next time
  • Smell figs before buying and skip any that smell sour
  • Refrigerate them right away

Dark spots or fuzzy mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeSmall round brown or black spots, or white, pink or black fuzz, often at a split in the skin.

Why it happens

Alternaria rot shows up as small, round, brown-to-black spots, and black mold as dark flesh followed by white fuzz and black spores. Cracks in the skin let them in. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it. Figs are soft fruit, so mold can't be cut away.

Prevent it next time
  • Store figs in a single layer on a paper towel
  • Eat them within two or three days

Very soft, split or collapsing

AgeCook it soon

What you'll seeThe fig slumps, the skin splits and the flesh is very soft, with no sour smell.

Why it happens

Figs keep softening after harvest. Ethylene speeds up softening and decay, especially at 41°F and above. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if it smells sweet, with no sour smell, ooze or mold. Eat or cook it today.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't stack figs
  • Keep them in the coldest part of the fridge
Use it up
  • Roast them with honey
  • Simmer them into a quick jam

What went wrong?

Figs and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Figs give off a moderate amount of ethylene as they ripen, enough to speed up sensitive produce in a closed drawer.
  • Figs are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 1% of figs by weight is usually trimmed off as stems (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Roast soft figs with honey for toast or yogurt
  • Compost the stems
