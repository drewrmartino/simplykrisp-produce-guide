How to wash strawberries and other berries, and whether vinegar helps

Short answer

Wash strawberries right before you eat them, not before storing. Rinse them in a colander under cool running water, then pat them dry. You don't need soap or a produce wash, and the FDA recommends against both. A vinegar soak is optional, not required.

How to wash strawberries

  1. Wait until you're about to eat them. Moisture helps mold grow, so berries keep longer if they stay dry until then.1
  2. Put them in a colander and rinse with cool running water, turning them as you go. A sink sprayer works well for soft fruit.21
  3. Use cool water, not hot. Hot water can make produce draw water, and any bacteria with it, into the fruit.2
  4. Pat them dry with a clean towel or paper towel. Drying also removes more bacteria.3
  5. Hull them and eat or use them. Put any washed leftovers back in the fridge, dry, and eat them soon.

Do you need a vinegar wash?

No. The FDA says plain running water is enough and recommends against soap, detergent and commercial produce washes. Produce is porous, and residue can be absorbed.43

A vinegar soak is optional. University of Maine Extension describes ½ cup distilled vinegar to 2 cups water for five minutes, followed by a rinse in clean water. They write about leafy greens: it reduces bacteria but can leave a vinegar taste and change the texture.1 Vinegar soaks are popular for berries too, but the evidence that they add days is mostly informal. If you try it, rinse afterward and dry the berries very well, because leftover moisture works against you.

Raspberries, blackberries and blueberries

  • Treat them the same way: rinse them in a colander right before eating, and don't soak them. Raspberries and blackberries are especially fragile.1
  • If you're freezing blueberries, don't wash them first. Washing makes the skins tougher. Rinse them when you use them.5

Why berries mold so fast

Gray mold (Botrytis) is the main reason strawberries get thrown out, and it keeps growing slowly even at 32°F.6 Washing doesn't stop it, and wet berries in a closed box help it spread. Store them dry, cold and in a single layer if you can. See why strawberries go bad.

Common questions

Should you wash strawberries before putting them in the fridge?

No. Wash them right before eating. Moisture helps mold grow, so berries last longer when they stay dry.1

Does soaking strawberries in vinegar make them last longer?

It's optional. A diluted vinegar soak can reduce bacteria on produce, but it can affect taste and texture, and the FDA says plain water is enough. If you try it, rinse and dry the berries thoroughly.14

Can I wash strawberries with soap or produce wash?

No. The FDA recommends against soap, detergent and commercial produce washes. Produce is porous and can absorb them.4

Why do my strawberries mold so fast?

Gray mold grows even at fridge temperature, and moisture helps it spread. Keep berries dry until you eat them, and remove any moldy ones right away.6
