How to ripen bananas faster, or keep them from ripening

Short answer

To ripen bananas faster, put them in a paper bag with an apple or another ripe banana and keep them somewhere warm. To slow them down once they're ripe, put them in the fridge. The peel turns dark, but the fruit inside stays good for a few more days.

How to ripen green bananas faster

  1. Put them in a paper bag with an apple or an already ripe banana. Ripening fruit gives off ethylene, the gas that triggers banana ripening, and the bag holds it in.1
  2. Keep the bag somewhere warm. UC Davis ripens bananas at 59 to 68°F, and warmer rooms ripen them faster.1
  3. Check them every day. Once yellow, they keep ripening on their own.

Never refrigerate green bananas

Bananas are tropical and are damaged below about 56°F. In the cold, the peel discolors, and unripe bananas may never ripen properly.1 Let them ripen on the counter first.

How to slow down ripe bananas

  • Put ripe bananas in the fridge. Cold damages the peel and turns it brown or black,1 but the fruit inside stays good for a couple of extra days. This is the one time the fridge makes sense.
  • Keep them away from your other produce. Bananas give off ethylene as they ripen and can speed up fruit stored next to them.
  • Wrapping the stems or separating the bunch is a popular tip. We haven't found good evidence that it makes much difference, so try it if you like, but don't count on it.
  • If you can't eat them in time, freeze them. See the freezing chart.

What the brown spots mean

Brown freckles on the peel mean the banana is ripening. It's driven by its own ethylene, and it happens faster in a warm room.1 A spotty banana is sweeter and softer. It's ideal for baking or smoothies, not a reason to throw it out. A banana that's leaking, moldy or smells fermented is done.

Does a Krisp help? Bananas belong on the counter, and a Krisp works inside the closed crisper drawer, so it can't reach them. Keep bananas away from your other produce instead. They give off ethylene too.

Common questions

Should bananas be refrigerated?

Not while they're green or still ripening, because cold damages them and can stop ripening. Once ripe, the fridge buys a few extra days. The peel darkens, but the fruit is fine.1

Why do bananas turn brown?

On the counter, brown spots mean they're ripening, driven by their own ethylene. In the fridge, a brown or black peel is cold damage to the skin.1

Do bananas make other fruit ripen faster?

Yes. Ripening bananas give off ethylene, which speeds up other fruit in a closed bag or bowl. That's why a banana in the bag helps ripen an avocado.12

Can you freeze bananas?

Yes. The National Center for Home Food Preservation recommends peeling and mashing them with a little ascorbic acid (vitamin C powder) to limit browning.3
