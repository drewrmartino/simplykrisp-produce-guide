How to ripen avocados fast, and how to slow them down

Short answer

Put the avocado in a closed paper bag with an apple or banana and leave it on the counter. Those fruits give off ethylene, the ripening gas, and the bag holds it around the avocado. Check it once a day with a gentle squeeze, and move it to the fridge as soon as it's ripe.

The fastest way to ripen an avocado

  1. Put the avocado in a paper bag with an apple, banana or kiwi. All three give off ethylene, which speeds ripening.1
  2. Fold the bag closed and leave it on the counter at room temperature, out of direct sun. Avocados don't ripen on the tree. They only start making their own ethylene after they're picked, and room temperature is where that happens.2
  3. Check once a day. A nearly ripe avocado usually turns soft-firm within about three days at room temperature, and sometimes by the second day.1
  4. Once it's ripe, eat it or move it to the fridge to slow it down.12

How to tell when an avocado is ripe

Hold it in your palm and squeeze gently. Don't press with your fingertips, which bruises the flesh.1

  • Ripe: it gives easily to gentle pressure, springs back a little, and the skin is a shade darker than an unripe one.1
  • Firm-ripe: it gives a little but is still firmer than ripe. It's good for slicing, and it'll be fully ripe the next day on the counter.1
  • Not ready: it's hard and doesn't give at all. Bag it as above.
  • Too far: it's mushy or the skin feels loose. See what went wrong with your avocado to check whether it's still good.

Don't put a hard avocado in the fridge

Cold stops an unripe avocado from ripening properly. UC Davis reports that unripe avocados held at 37 to 41°F for more than two weeks can turn gray or brown inside and may never ripen.2 Ripen it on the counter first, and refrigerate it only once it's ripe.

How to slow down a ripe avocado

  • Keep it away from apples, bananas and kiwis, which speed it up.1
  • Put ripe avocados in the fridge. UC Davis stores ripe fruit at 36 to 40°F, which is normal fridge temperature.2
  • Plan on eating a ripe, refrigerated avocado within a few days.

Skip the water hack

Storing whole or cut avocados under water in the fridge is a popular social media tip, but it isn't safe. In FDA sampling, about 18% of avocado skins carried Listeria.3 In an FDA lab study, Listeria got into the flesh of whole avocados held in contaminated water.4 Store avocados dry, and for a cut one, see how to store a cut avocado.

Why the fruit gets no Krisp note: Ripe avocados give off a lot of ethylene, and once one is ripening, less ethylene around it changes little. Store ripe avocados apart from greens and other sensitive produce instead.

Common questions

Does a paper bag really ripen avocados faster?

Yes, especially with an apple, banana or kiwi in the bag. Those fruits give off ethylene, and a closed bag keeps it around the avocado.1

Should avocados be refrigerated?

Only once they're ripe. Cold keeps an unripe avocado from ripening properly and can brown it inside, but it slows down a ripe one.2

How long does it take an avocado to ripen?

A nearly ripe avocado usually turns soft-firm within about three days on the counter. A rock-hard one takes longer, and a bag with an apple or banana speeds it up.1

Is it safe to store avocados in water?

No. FDA sampling found Listeria on about 18% of avocado skins, and FDA researchers found that it can get into the flesh of avocados held in water.34
