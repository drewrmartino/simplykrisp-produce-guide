How to wash vegetables and lettuce

Short answer

Rinse vegetables under cool running water and rub them with your hands. Scrub firm ones like potatoes, carrots and cucumbers with a clean brush. Separate leafy greens and soak them in cool water, then lift them out and dry them. Bagged greens labeled "pre-washed" don't need washing again.

The basics for any vegetable

  1. Wash your hands for 20 seconds with soap and warm water.1
  2. Cut away bruised or damaged spots.1
  3. Rinse before you peel or cut, so the knife doesn't carry dirt into the part you eat.12
  4. Rub the vegetable under cool running water. Don't use soap or a produce wash.1
  5. Dry it with a clean cloth or paper towel.1

By type of vegetable

VegetableHow to wash it
Lettuce, spinach, kale and other greensPull off the outer leaves of a head.1 Separate the leaves and soak them in cool water for a few minutes, then lift them out, drain and repeat. Dry them in a salad spinner or on paper towels.3
Broccoli and cauliflowerSoak for 1 to 2 minutes in cool water, then drain and dry.3
Potatoes, carrots, cucumbers and other firm vegetablesScrub with a clean vegetable brush under running water.13
MushroomsWait until right before cooking. Moisture makes them spoil faster.3
Fresh herbsRinse them in a colander under a sink sprayer, turning them as you go.4
Bagged salad labeled pre-washed or ready-to-eatNo washing needed. If you rinse it anyway, keep it away from unwashed surfaces.2

What not to use

  • Soap, detergent or bleach. Produce is porous and can absorb them, even after rinsing.2
  • Commercial produce washes. The FDA says their residues haven't been shown to be safe and their effectiveness hasn't been tested.2
  • Very cold water on warm produce. Colorado State suggests rinse water no more than 10°F colder than the produce, so the produce doesn't draw water in through the stem end.5

Wash now or later?

For anything that molds or turns slimy, like berries, mushrooms and tender greens, wait to wash until you're ready to use it.3 If you wash greens ahead for the week, dry them very well before they go back in the fridge, since trapped moisture is what makes greens slimy. See why lettuce goes bad.

Common questions

Do I need to wash organic or farmers market produce?

Yes. The FDA recommends washing all produce under running water, including homegrown, farmers market and grocery store produce.2

Should I wash vegetables I'm going to peel?

Yes. Wash before peeling so dirt and bacteria on the skin aren't carried onto the part you eat.2

Does a vinegar soak clean vegetables better?

It can reduce bacteria on leafy greens, but it can leave a vinegar taste and change the texture. University of Maine Extension suggests ½ cup vinegar to 2 cups water for five minutes, followed by a rinse. Plain water is what the FDA recommends.31

Do I need to wash bagged salad?

Not if the bag says pre-washed or ready-to-eat.2
