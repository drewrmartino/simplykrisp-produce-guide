How to store plums, and why they go bad

Plums ripen on the counter and bruise less easily than peaches. The usual problems are flesh that browns after a cold stay, fruit left too long, and mold.

Where to keep them
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long they last
3-5 days ripe (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 31°F (-1°C)
Don't store next to
Apples and bananas
What are you seeing?
Brown, glassy or mealy fleshVery soft and wrinkledBrown soft rot or fuzzy mold

How to store plums

  • Ripen on the counter until slightly soft
  • Once ripe, refrigerate in the crisper drawer
  • Store in a single layer to prevent bruising
  • Do not wash until ready to eat

Picking good plums: Slightly soft to the touch with a sweet fragrance. The skin should have a powdery bloom.

Does a Krisp help? Tested directly: continuous ethylene had no effect on plums in cold storage.

Why plums go bad

Brown, glassy or mealy flesh

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeThe flesh looks translucent or brown, tastes flat and feels mealy, even though the skin looks fine.

Why it happens

This is internal breakdown, a kind of chilling injury. Plums held at 36 to 46°F are the most prone to it, and that range includes most fridges. It shows up later, as the fruit ripens. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no mold. The flavor and texture are poor, so cook them.

Prevent it next time
  • Ripen plums on the counter until slightly soft
  • Refrigerate only ripe plums, and eat them within three to five days
Use it up
  • Roast them with a little sugar
  • Simmer them into jam or a sauce

Very soft and wrinkled

OverripeningCook it soon

What you'll seeThe plum gives easily all over, and the skin wrinkles or splits.

Why it happens

Plums keep ripening after harvest and make far more ethylene once ripe and warm than they do cold. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if it smells sweet and there's no mold or leaking. Use it today.

Prevent it next time
  • Move ripe plums to the crisper drawer
  • Store them in a single layer
Use it up
  • Simmer them into a quick compote
  • Bake them into a cake or crumble

Brown soft rot or fuzzy mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft brown spot that spreads, often with tan, gray or white fuzz.

Why it happens

Brown rot, gray mold and Rhizopus rot are the main plum diseases after harvest. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it. Plums are soft, juicy fruit, so mold can spread below the surface.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't wash plums until you eat them
  • Remove any rotting plum right away

What went wrong?

Plums and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Plums give off a moderate amount of ethylene as they ripen, enough to speed up sensitive produce in a closed drawer.
  • Plums are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep them apart from apples and bananas.

The part people throw away

About 6% of plums by weight is usually trimmed off as pits and skins (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Simmer leftover skins with sugar for a quick plum syrup
  • Compost the pits. Never crack or eat the kernels
