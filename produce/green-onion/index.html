How to store green onions, and why they go bad

Green onions last a week or two. The tops yellow and go limp first, and bruised or wet ones turn slimy.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
1-2 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Yellow topsLimp, wilted topsSlimy leaves

How to store green onions

  • Stand upright in a jar with an inch of water and cover loosely with a bag
  • Change the water every few days
  • Alternatively, wrap in a damp paper towel and place in a bag
  • Trim and freeze for long-term storage

Picking good green onions: Firm white bulbs with crisp, bright green tops. Avoid any with slimy or yellowed greens.

Does a Krisp help? Green onions don't react to ethylene from other produce. Warmth and age are what yellow the tops, so keeping them cold and damp does more than a Krisp.

Why green onions go bad

Yellow tops

AgeTrim and use

What you'll seeThe green tops turn yellow, starting at the tips.

Why it happens

The tops yellow as they age. UC Davis notes that higher temperatures greatly speed it up. 1

Is it still OK?

Cut off the yellow parts. The rest is fine if it's firm and not slimy.

Prevent it next time
  • Refrigerate green onions as soon as you get home
  • Use them within one to two weeks
Use it up
  • Slice the good parts and freeze them
  • Cook them into fried rice, eggs or soup

Limp, wilted tops

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe tops droop and feel soft.

Why it happens

Green onions lose water quickly. UC Davis calls preventing water loss critical and recommends above 98 percent humidity. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if there's no slime or bad smell.

Prevent it next time
  • Stand them upright in a jar with an inch of water and cover them loosely with a bag
  • Or wrap them in a damp paper towel inside a bag
Use it up
  • Trim the ends and stand them in cold water for an hour
  • Cook them

Slimy leaves

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeThe leaves or outer layers turn dark, wet and slimy, and smell bad.

Why it happens

This is bacterial soft rot, sometimes with gray mold. Bruising is common in green onions and leads to rapid decay. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss slimy or bad-smelling green onions. FDA says to throw away produce that looks rotten. Check the rest of the bunch, and use the firm, fresh-smelling ones today.

Prevent it next time
  • Change the water every few days if you keep them in a jar
  • Don't crush the bunch under other produce

What went wrong?

Green onions and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Green onions give off only a little ethylene, so they are fine next to most other produce.
  • Green onions are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 4% of green onions by weight is usually trimmed off as root ends (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Stand the roots in a glass of water and regrow 2–3 harvests
  • Compost when they tire out
