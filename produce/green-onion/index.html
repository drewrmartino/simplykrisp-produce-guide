How to store green onions

Green onions keep best in the fridge, at about 32°F, and last 1-2 weeks. Here is how to store them, what to keep them away from, and how to pick good green onions.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
1-2 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular

How to store green onions

  • Stand upright in a jar with an inch of water and cover loosely with a bag
  • Change the water every few days
  • Alternatively, wrap in a damp paper towel and place in a bag
  • Trim and freeze for long-term storage

Picking good green onions: Firm white bulbs with crisp, bright green tops. Avoid any with slimy or yellowed greens.

Does a Krisp help? A Krisp makes a real difference here. Slows the green tops yellowing. Expect about 2–3 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Green onions and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Green onions give off only a little ethylene, so they are fine next to most other produce.
  • Green onions are very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast they age.

The part people throw away

About 4% of green onions by weight is usually trimmed off as root ends (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Stand the roots in a glass of water and regrow 2–3 harvests
  • Compost when they tire out
