How to store cherries, and why they go bad

Cherries don't ripen after picking and barely react to ethylene. They fail from mold, from bruising and from drying out.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
5-7 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 31°F (-1°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Brown soft spots or fuzzy moldDents and soft bruisesShriveled fruit and dry, brown stems

How to store cherries

  • Store unwashed in a breathable bag or container
  • Keep stems attached until ready to eat
  • Do not stack too deep — they bruise easily
  • Bring to room temperature before eating for best flavor

Picking good cherries: Firm, glossy, and deeply colored with green stems still attached.

Does a Krisp help? Tested directly: continuous ethylene had no effect on sweet cherries in cold storage.

Why cherries go bad

Brown soft spots or fuzzy mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft brown spot that spreads, or gray or white fuzz, often starting near the stem or at a split.

Why it happens

Brown rot, gray mold and Rhizopus rot are the main cherry diseases after harvest. Gray mold keeps growing slowly even at 32°F, and Rhizopus shows up in fruit kept at 41°F or warmer. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss moldy or rotting cherries and any soft or wet ones touching them. You can't cut mold out of soft fruit.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep cherries cold and dry, and don't wash them until you eat them
  • Pick out split or damaged cherries when you get home

Dents and soft bruises

BruisingStill good

What you'll seeSmall sunken dimples in the skin, or soft flat spots.

Why it happens

Pitting is a dent left when cells under the skin collapse after an impact. Bruises come from pressure and drops. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if there's no mold or leaking. Eat the dented ones first.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't pile cherries deep or put anything on top of them
  • Leave the stems on until you eat them
Use it up
  • Pit and freeze them for smoothies
  • Simmer them into a quick sauce

Shriveled fruit and dry, brown stems

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe skin wrinkles, the fruit softens, and the stems turn brown and brittle.

Why it happens

Cherries lose water in dry air. UC Davis lists shriveling as a quality defect and recommends 90 to 95 percent humidity. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. They are a little less juicy.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them in a bag or covered container in the fridge
Use it up
  • Pit and bake them into a clafoutis or a crisp

What went wrong?

Cherries and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Cherries give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Cherries are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 8% of cherries by weight is usually trimmed off as pits and stems (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Dry the stems and steep as a mild tea
  • Wash and dry pits to fill a microwavable heat pack. Never crack or eat the kernels
