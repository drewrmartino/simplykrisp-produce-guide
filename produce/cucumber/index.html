Cucumbers: what went wrong, and what to do

Cucumbers are tricky: a regular fridge is colder than they like, and they're sensitive to ethylene too. Most problems come from those two things.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
1 week (refrigerated)
Keep it away from
Apples, Bananas, Tomatoes and Cantaloupe

Watery spots and pits in the skin

Too coldTrim and use

What you'll seeSunken pits or dark, water-soaked patches on the skin, sometimes followed by soft, rotting spots.

Why it happens

This is chilling injury. Cucumbers are damaged below 50°F if they stay there more than one to three days, and most fridges run around 37°F to 40°F. The damage adds up over time and makes rot more likely. 1

Is it still OK?

If the cucumber is still firm, cut off the pitted spots and use it soon. If it's soft, slimy or moldy, toss it.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them in the warmest part of the fridge, in the door or at the front
  • Wrap each one in a paper towel, then loosely in a bag
  • Buy only what you'll use within a few days
Use it up
  • Make quick refrigerator pickles
  • Grate it into tzatziki

Skin turning yellow

Ethylene gasStill good

What you'll seeGreen skin fades to yellow, and the cucumber may get seedy or bitter.

Why it happens

Cucumbers are highly sensitive to ethylene. 1 to 5 parts per million speeds up yellowing and decay, which is why UC Davis says not to store them with bananas, melons or tomatoes. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. Peel it if the skin tastes bitter.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them away from bananas, melons, tomatoes and apples
Use it up
  • Peel and blend it into gazpacho or a smoothie
  • Make quick pickles
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp makes a real difference here. Slows yellowing and soft, watery spots. Expect about 2–3 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Soft, slimy or moldy

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeThe cucumber goes soft and mushy, leaks, feels slimy, or shows mold.

Why it happens

Bacteria and fungi cause most cucumber losses, especially after chilling injury has weakened the skin. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it. USDA lists cucumbers as soft produce, where mold can spread below the surface, so cutting it away isn't enough.

Prevent it next time
  • Avoid the coldest parts of the fridge
  • Keep them dry

How to store cucumbers

  • Store in the warmest part of the fridge (door or front)
  • Wrap individually in a paper towel, then loosely in a bag
  • Cucumbers are sensitive to cold — too cold causes water-soaked spots
  • Keep away from ethylene-producing fruits

Full storage details are in the Produce Storage Guide.

The part people throw away

About 3% of cucumbers by weight is usually trimmed off as ends and peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Drop ends into a water pitcher
  • Blend peels into a green smoothie
