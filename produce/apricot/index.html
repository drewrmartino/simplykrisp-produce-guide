How to store apricots, and why they go bad

Apricots are fragile. They bruise at a touch once soft, turn brown and spongy after a cold stay, and rot quickly.

Where to keep them
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long they last
3-5 days ripe (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 31°F (-1°C)
Don't store next to
Apples and bananas
What are you seeing?
Brown, spongy or gel-like fleshSoft, brown bruisesBrown soft rot or fuzzy mold

How to store apricots

  • Ripen at room temperature until slightly soft
  • Once ripe, refrigerate immediately
  • Store in a single layer to prevent bruising
  • Handle gently — they bruise very easily

Picking good apricots: Slightly soft to the touch with a deep golden-orange color and a sweet fragrance.

Does a Krisp help? Stone fruit showed no measurable response to ethylene over three to eight weeks of cold storage.

Why apricots go bad

Brown, spongy or gel-like flesh

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeWatery patches in the flesh that turn brown, with a spongy or jelly-like texture and little flavor.

Why it happens

UC Davis calls this gel breakdown or chilling injury. Apricots stored at 36 to 46°F, the range most fridges run in, have a short life and lose flavor. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no mold and it smells fresh. Cook them.

Prevent it next time
  • Ripen apricots on the counter until slightly soft
  • Refrigerate only ripe apricots, and eat them within three to five days
Use it up
  • Simmer them into jam or a compote
  • Roast them with honey

Soft, brown bruises

BruisingTrim and use

What you'll seeFlat, soft, dark areas where the fruit was pressed.

Why it happens

Soft apricots bruise very easily. 12

Is it still OK?

Cut the bruise out and eat the rest today. Toss the apricot if the bruise is moldy or smells sour.

Prevent it next time
  • Store them in a single layer
  • Handle ripe apricots as little as possible
Use it up
  • Chop the good parts into oatmeal or yogurt

Brown soft rot or fuzzy mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft brown spot that spreads, often with tan, gray or white fuzz.

Why it happens

Brown rot and Rhizopus rot are the main apricot diseases after harvest. 13

Is it still OK?

Toss it. Apricots are soft, juicy fruit, so mold can spread below the surface.

Prevent it next time
  • Remove any rotting apricot right away
  • Don't wash apricots until you eat them

What went wrong?

Apricots and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Apricots give off a moderate amount of ethylene as they ripen, enough to speed up sensitive produce in a closed drawer.
  • Apricots are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep them apart from apples and bananas.

The part people throw away

About 7% of apricots by weight is usually trimmed off as pits (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Compost the pits. Never crack or eat the kernels
  • Cook soft apricots into jam
