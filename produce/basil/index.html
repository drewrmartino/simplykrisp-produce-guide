How to store basil, and why it goes bad

Basil is the one common herb that hates the fridge. Cold turns it black, and on the counter it wilts within a week.

Where to keep it
Counter
How long it lasts
5-7 days (counter)
Best temperature
About 55°F (13°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Leaves turning blackWilted, droopy leavesGray fuzz or slimy leaves

How to store basil

  • Trim the stems and stand the bunch in a glass of water on the counter, out of direct sun
  • Do not refrigerate it. Basil leaves turn black in the cold
  • Change the water every day or two
  • Blend extra leaves into pesto and freeze it

Picking good basil: Glossy, bright green leaves with no black spots, and a strong smell when you brush them.

Does a Krisp help? Basil lives on the counter because the fridge blackens it, so it never shares a drawer with a Krisp. It barely reacts to ethylene anyway.

Why basil goes bad

Leaves turning black

Too coldTrim and use

What you'll seeLeaves get dark brown or black patches, often within a day or two of going in the fridge.

Why it happens

Basil is damaged by cold, and UC Davis says to keep it above 50°F. Research has found basil leaves blacken in as little as one day at 32°F, or three days at 41°F. 12

Is it still OK?

Cold damage isn't a safety problem, but black leaves won't recover. Pick them off and use the rest today.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep basil on the counter, stems in a glass of water, out of direct sun
Use it up
  • Blend the good leaves into pesto and freeze it in an ice-cube tray

Wilted, droopy leaves

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeLeaves hang limp but are still green.

Why it happens

Basil has large, thin leaves that lose water fast. UC Davis says how long it lasts depends mostly on water loss. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. Trim the stems and put them in fresh water. It often perks up within an hour.

Prevent it next time
  • Stand the stems in water like cut flowers
  • Keep it out of direct sun and away from the stove
Use it up
  • Make pesto
  • Tear it into tomato sauce at the end of cooking

Gray fuzz or slimy leaves

MoldToss it

What you'll seeLeaves turn dark and slimy, or grow gray fuzzy mold.

Why it happens

UC Davis notes that gray mold is common on basil, and damaged or wet leaves are where it starts. 13

Is it still OK?

Pick out and toss the moldy or slimy stems. If the whole bunch smells off, toss it.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the leaves dry. Only the stems should touch water
  • Change the water every day or two

What went wrong?

Basil and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Basil gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Basil is barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 36% of basil by weight is usually trimmed off as stems and flower heads (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Steep stems in warm olive oil or simmer them in tomato sauce, then fish them out
  • Root a cut stem in a glass of water for a new plant
