How to store honeydew, and why it goes bad

Honeydew is damaged by fridge temperatures unless it's already ripe. An unripe one that goes in cold may never ripen, and cut melon needs to be eaten within a few days.

Where to keep it
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long it lasts
5-7 days ripe (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 50°F (10°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas and peaches
What are you seeing?
Tan or reddish patches on the rindMold on the rindCut melon turned slimy or sour

How to store honeydew

  • Store whole melon on the counter until ripe
  • Once ripe, refrigerate whole or cut
  • Wrap cut melon tightly in plastic
  • Keep away from ethylene-producing fruits

Picking good honeydew: Slightly waxy feel, creamy yellow skin (not white), and a faint sweet smell at the blossom end.

Does a Krisp help? Whole melons ripen on the counter and are too big for the drawer. Once cut, wrap it and eat it within a few days.

Why honeydew goes bad

Tan or reddish patches on the rind

Too coldStill good

What you'll seeReddish-tan blotches or small pits on the rind after time in the fridge. The melon may stay hard and bland.

Why it happens

Honeydew is damaged by several days below 45°F, and most fridges run around 37°F to 40°F. The rind pits and discolors, the melon may fail to ripen, and it picks up off-flavors and decays more easily. 1

Is it still OK?

Cut it open. If the flesh looks and smells normal, it's fine to eat, though it may be bland. Toss it if the rind is soft or moldy or the flesh smells off.

Prevent it next time
  • Ripen whole honeydew on the counter
  • Refrigerate it only once it's ripe, and eat it within a few days
Use it up
  • Blend bland melon with lime juice and a little honey
  • Freeze cubes for smoothies

Mold on the rind

Trapped moistureToss it

What you'll seeGray, black or pink fuzzy patches on the rind, usually underneath or at the stem end.

Why it happens

Surface molds grow on honeydew when the rind stays damp. UC Davis notes that long periods of high humidity or condensation encourage them. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss a melon with mold or soft rot on the rind. FDA says to throw away produce that looks rotten, and a knife carries what's on the rind into the flesh.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the melon dry, and wipe off condensation when it comes out of the fridge
  • Don't leave it sitting in a wet spot or a sealed bag

Cut melon turned slimy or sour

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeCut pieces feel slick and smell sour or fermented.

Why it happens

Cut melon is wet and exposed, so bacteria can grow on it. FDA notes that a knife can carry dirt and bacteria from the rind onto the flesh. 21

Is it still OK?

Toss it. Don't taste cut melon that is slimy or smells off.

Prevent it next time
  • Scrub the rind under running water with a clean brush before cutting
  • Wrap cut melon tightly and refrigerate it right away
  • Eat cut melon within a few days

What went wrong?

Honeydew and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Honeydew gives off a moderate amount of ethylene as it ripens, enough to speed up sensitive produce in a closed drawer.
  • Honeydew is very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast it ages.

Keep it apart from apples, bananas and peaches.

The part people throw away

About 54% of honeydew by weight is usually trimmed off as rind and seeds (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Pickle the pale inner rind
  • Blend the seed cavity with water and strain for agua fresca
