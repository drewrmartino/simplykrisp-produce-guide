How to store mangoes, and why they go bad

Mangoes are tropical. Chilling them before they're ripe ruins them, and once ripe they have only a few days.

Where to keep them
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long they last
5-7 days ripe (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 56°F (13°C)
Don't store next to
Apples and bananas
What are you seeing?
Gray, dull skin and uneven ripeningBlack spots on the skinVery soft, wrinkled or stringy

How to store mangoes

  • Ripen on the counter at room temperature
  • Once ripe, refrigerate to extend freshness by several days
  • To speed ripening, place in a paper bag
  • Store cut mango in an airtight container in the fridge

Picking good mangoes: A ripe mango gives slightly when squeezed and has a fruity aroma at the stem end. Color is not a reliable indicator.

Does a Krisp help? Mangoes ripen on the counter, and once ripe they have only a day or two. A Krisp adds little.

Why mangoes go bad

Gray, dull skin and uneven ripening

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeGrayish, scald-like patches or pits on the skin, and fruit that ripens unevenly with poor color and flavor.

Why it happens

Unripe mangoes are damaged by cold. UC Davis recommends 55°F for mature-green mangoes and 50°F for ripe ones, both well above fridge temperature. Chilled mangoes ripen unevenly, taste flat and decay more easily, and the flesh sometimes browns. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if the flesh smells fresh and has no mold. The flavor will be flat.

Prevent it next time
  • Ripen mangoes on the counter
  • Refrigerate only ripe mangoes, and eat them within five to seven days
Use it up
  • Blend it into a smoothie or lassi with lime
  • Cook it into chutney

Black spots on the skin

MoldTrim and use

What you'll seeDark spots that grow as the mango ripens, or a black ring around the stem.

Why it happens

Anthracnose is a fungal disease that shows up as dark spots on the skin and can darken the flesh underneath. Stem-end rot starts as a round black patch around the stem. 123

Is it still OK?

Spots that stay in the skin come off when you peel it. If the flesh underneath is dark, soft or smells off, or you see fuzzy mold, toss the mango.

Prevent it next time
  • Choose mangoes without dark sunken spots or a black stem end
  • Eat ripe mangoes promptly, because the spots grow as the fruit softens
Use it up
  • Peel it, check the flesh, and cube the good fruit for the freezer

Very soft, wrinkled or stringy

OverripeningCook it soon

What you'll seeThe mango gives easily all over, the skin wrinkles, and the flesh is very soft.

Why it happens

Mangoes keep ripening after harvest, and they give off more ethylene the warmer they are. A ripe mango on the counter goes past its best quickly. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if it smells sweet and fruity. Toss it if it smells sour or like alcohol, or the flesh has turned gray or brown.

Prevent it next time
  • Move ripe mangoes to the fridge
  • Cube and freeze ripe mango you will not eat in time
Use it up
  • Blend it into a smoothie
  • Cook it down into a sauce for yogurt or ice cream

What went wrong?

Mangoes and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Mangoes give off a moderate amount of ethylene as they ripen, enough to speed up sensitive produce in a closed drawer.
  • Mangoes are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep them apart from apples and bananas.

The part people throw away

About 29% of mangoes by weight is usually trimmed off as pit and skin (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Scrape the fruit left on the pit into a smoothie
  • Mango skin can irritate people sensitive to poison ivy, so compost it
