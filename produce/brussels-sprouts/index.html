How to store Brussels sprouts, and why they go bad

Brussels sprouts are very sensitive to ethylene. The outer leaves yellow and fall off first, then the sprouts go limp, and old or bruised ones turn dark and slimy.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
3-5 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, pears and tomatoes
What are you seeing?
Yellow, loose outer leavesSoft, limp sproutsDark, slimy leaves

How to store Brussels sprouts

  • Store in a breathable bag in the crisper drawer
  • Do not wash until ready to cook
  • Keep on the stalk if possible for longer freshness
  • Remove any yellowed outer leaves before storing

Picking good Brussels sprouts: Tight, compact heads that are bright green with no yellowing.

Why Brussels sprouts go bad

Yellow, loose outer leaves

Ethylene gasTrim and use

What you'll seeThe outer leaves turn yellow and come away from the sprout.

Why it happens

Ethylene makes Brussels sprouts yellow and shed their outer leaves. They make little of it themselves, so the source is usually ripening fruit nearby. 1

Is it still OK?

Peel off the yellow leaves. The sprout underneath is fine if it's firm and green.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them away from apples, pears and tomatoes
  • Store them in a breathable bag in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Roast them
  • Shred them for a slaw or a quick sauté
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp makes a real difference here. Keeps the outer leaves from yellowing. Expect about 2–3 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Soft, limp sprouts

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeSprouts feel soft and loose instead of tight and firm.

Why it happens

Without high humidity, sprouts lose water and go limp. UC Davis recommends above 95 percent humidity. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no slime or bad smell.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them in a bag in the crisper drawer
  • Buy them on the stalk when you can
Use it up
  • Roast them at high heat
  • Shred and sauté them

Dark, slimy leaves

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeDark, wet, slimy leaves and a strong bad smell.

Why it happens

This is bacterial soft rot. It usually follows bruising or other damage. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss sprouts that are slimy or smell bad. FDA says to throw away produce that looks rotten. Sort the rest and cook the firm ones soon.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't wash them until you cook them
  • Use them within three to five days

Brussels sprouts and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Brussels sprouts give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Brussels sprouts are very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast they age.

Keep them apart from apples, pears and tomatoes.

The part people throw away

About 10% of Brussels sprouts by weight is usually trimmed off as outer leaves (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Roast loose leaves with oil and salt as crispy chips
  • Toss them into a stir-fry
