How to store strawberries, and why they go bad

Mold is the number one reason strawberries get thrown out. Ethylene barely matters for them, but keeping them dry and cold does.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
3-7 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular

How to store strawberries

  • Do not wash until ready to eat — moisture causes mold
  • Store in a single layer on paper towels
  • Remove any moldy berries immediately
  • Keep stems on until eating

Picking good strawberries: Bright red all the way to the stem with a sweet aroma. Avoid berries with white or green tips.

Does a Krisp help? Berries barely react to ethylene. Mold is what ends them, so keep them dry and rinse just before eating.

Why strawberries go bad

Gray, fuzzy mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeGray fuzz, often starting on one berry and spreading to the ones touching it.

Why it happens

Gray mold (Botrytis) causes the most strawberry losses after harvest. It grows even at 32°F, just very slowly, and moisture on the berries helps it spread. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss moldy berries. Check the ones that were touching them, and toss any that are soft or wet. Strawberries are soft fruit, so you can't cut mold away.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't wash berries until you eat them
  • Store them in a single layer on paper towels
  • Pull out moldy berries as soon as you see them

Soft, mushy or leaking

BruisingCook it soon

What you'll seeBerries collapse, leak juice and stain the container, with no visible mold.

Why it happens

Crushed berries break down fast, and Rhizopus rot causes mushy, leaky berries. It doesn't grow below 41°F, so keeping them cold helps. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if there's no mold. Cook or blend them today.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them in the coldest part of the fridge
  • Don't stack heavy things on the container
Use it up
  • Cook them into a quick compote or jam
  • Freeze them for smoothies

Dull, shriveled berries

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeBerries lose their shine and start to shrivel.

Why it happens

Strawberries lose water through their thin skin, especially when the stems are pulled off or they're left uncovered. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. The texture is better in cooking or blended.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the green tops on until you eat them
  • Keep them covered in the fridge
Use it up
  • Roast them with a little sugar
  • Blend them into a smoothie

What went wrong?

Strawberries and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Strawberries give off only a little ethylene, so they are fine next to most other produce.
  • Strawberries are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 6% of strawberries by weight is usually trimmed off as tops (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Tops are edible: blend them into smoothies
  • Steep tops in water or vinegar overnight for strawberry water or vinegar
