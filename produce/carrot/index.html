How to store carrots, and why they go bad

Carrots last for weeks, so when they fail, it's usually because they dried out or picked up a bitter taste from ethylene. Both are fixable.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
3-4 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples
What are you seeing?
Bitter tasteBendy, rubbery carrotsWhite film on baby carrotsMoldy or slimy spots

How to store carrots

  • Remove green tops before storing — they draw moisture
  • Store in a plastic bag in the crisper drawer
  • Can be stored in water in the fridge for extra crispness
  • Keep away from ethylene-producing fruits to prevent bitterness

Picking good carrots: Firm, smooth, and bright orange. Avoid carrots that bend or have cracks.

Why carrots go bad

Bitter taste

Ethylene gasCook it soon

What you'll seeThe carrots look fine but taste bitter or soapy.

Why it happens

Ethylene makes carrots produce a bitter compound. As little as 0.5 parts per million can cause noticeable bitterness within two weeks. An apple in the same drawer is enough. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. Cooking them with something sweet balances the bitterness.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep carrots away from apples, pears and other fruit
  • Store them in a bag in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Roast or glaze them with honey or maple
  • Blend them into carrot-ginger soup
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp makes a real difference here. Stops the bitter taste carrots pick up from ethylene. Expect about 2–3 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Bendy, rubbery carrots

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeCarrots bend without snapping and may look shriveled.

Why it happens

Carrots go rubbery when they lose water. Green tops left on keep pulling moisture out of the root. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. Soak them in cold water for an hour to firm them back up.

Prevent it next time
  • Cut off green tops before storing
  • Keep them in a closed bag, or in a container of water in the fridge
Use it up
  • Grate them into slaw, muffins or fritters
  • Simmer them in soup or stock

White film on baby carrots

Drying outStill good

What you'll seePeeled baby carrots look chalky or whitish.

Why it happens

This is white blush: the cut, peeled surface drying out. It's cosmetic. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. Rinse them or soak them in water for a few minutes and the color comes back.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the bag sealed between uses

Moldy or slimy spots

MoldTrim and use

What you'll seeFuzzy patches, or soft, wet, slimy areas, often at the tip or crown.

Why it happens

Gray mold, watery rot and bacterial soft rot all attack carrots, and standing water in the bag helps them along. 12

Is it still OK?

Carrots are firm, so USDA says a small mold spot can be cut off with at least an inch around and below it. If a carrot is slimy or soft all the way through, toss it.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the bag free of standing water
  • Check stored carrots every week or so

What went wrong?

Carrots and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Carrots give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Carrots are very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast they age.

Keep them apart from apples.

The part people throw away

About 11% of carrots by weight is usually trimmed off as tops and peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Blend the tops into pesto or chimichurri
  • Add peels to a freezer stock bag
