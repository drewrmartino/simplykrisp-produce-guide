How to store artichokes, and why they go bad

Artichokes are flower buds, and they fade within a week. The leaves bruise, dry out and open up.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
5-7 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Brown or black marks on the leavesDry, spreading leavesGray mold or slimy spots

How to store artichokes

  • Store unwashed in a plastic bag in the crisper drawer
  • Sprinkle a few drops of water in the bag to keep the leaves from drying out
  • Do not trim or cut them until you cook them. Cut surfaces brown quickly
  • Cook them within a week. The flavor fades the longer they sit

Picking good artichokes: Heavy for its size with tight, squeaky leaves. A little brown on the tips is fine. Spreading, dry leaves mean it is old.

Does a Krisp help? Artichokes barely react to ethylene. They dry out and bruise, so a bag in the drawer does more than a Krisp.

Why artichokes go bad

Brown or black marks on the leaves

BruisingStill good

What you'll seeLeaf tips and scuffed areas turn brown or black.

Why it happens

UC Davis calls bruising and compression injury very common in artichokes. The damaged spots darken. 1

Is it still OK?

Cosmetic. The heart and the base of the leaves are fine if the artichoke is still heavy and firm.

Prevent it next time
  • Handle them gently and do not pack them under heavy produce
  • Store them in a bag so they do not rub against other items
Use it up
  • Steam or braise them
  • Trim the tips with scissors before cooking

Dry, spreading leaves

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeThe artichoke feels light, and the leaves spread open and turn tough.

Why it happens

Artichokes lose water and quality quickly. UC Davis notes that their look and eating quality decline rapidly, with under three weeks of storage even in ideal commercial conditions. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there is no mold or slime. The outer leaves will be tough, so trim more of them.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them in a plastic bag in the crisper drawer
  • Cook them within a week
Use it up
  • Use the hearts in pasta or dip

Gray mold or slimy spots

MoldToss it

What you'll seeGray fuzz between the leaves, or soft, wet, dark patches.

Why it happens

Gray mold and bacterial soft rot are the main decay problems UC Davis lists for artichokes. Both take hold when they are stored too warm. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss an artichoke with mold or slime. The layered leaves make it impossible to see how far it goes.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them cold
  • Store them unwashed

Artichokes and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Artichokes give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Artichokes are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 60% of artichokes by weight is usually trimmed off as outer leaves, choke and stem (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Peel the stem and cook it with the heart. It tastes the same
  • Simmer the tough outer leaves into a broth for risotto or soup
