What usually causes it

Across these 15 items, ethylene is only part of the picture, which is why storage habits matter as much as any product.

  • Drying outProduce keeps losing water after harvest. Dry fridge air speeds it up, and nothing replaces what is lost.11 of 49
  • Ethylene gasA ripening gas given off by apples, bananas, avocados, tomatoes and other fruit. Sensitive produce yellows, spots or turns bitter around it.9 of 49
  • MoldFungi such as gray mold. Some grow slowly even at refrigerator temperatures.7 of 49
  • Too coldTropical and warm-season produce is damaged by temperatures that are normal for a fridge, even without freezing.6 of 49
  • Bacterial rotSoft-rot bacteria break tissue down into wet, slimy patches, usually starting where it was bruised, cut or frozen.5 of 49
  • BruisingBumps and crushing break cells open. The damaged spot browns and is where rot gets in.4 of 49
  • OverripeningThe fruit keeps ripening after you buy it, driven by its own ethylene, until it goes past its best.3 of 49
  • AgeProduce is still alive after harvest. It slowly uses up its stored sugar and water, even when stored well.2 of 49
  • Trapped moistureWet surfaces and sealed containers give bacteria and mold the damp conditions they need.2 of 49
