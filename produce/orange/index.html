How to store oranges, and why they go bad

Oranges are hardy and keep for weeks in the fridge. Mold is the main way they fail. Otherwise they slowly dry out.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
3-4 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 45°F (7°C)
Don't store next to
Apples
What are you seeing?

How to store oranges

  • Store in the refrigerator for maximum freshness
  • Can sit on the counter for about a week
  • Do not store in sealed plastic bags — they need some air
  • Keep away from high ethylene producers

Picking good oranges: Choose oranges that are firm, heavy for their size, and have smooth skin.

Does a Krisp help? Citrus barely reacts to ethylene, so a Krisp won't change much. It keeps for weeks in the fridge anyway.

Why oranges go bad

Soft spot with green or blue mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft, watery patch that grows white fuzz and then green or blue powder, or a sour-smelling soft rot.

Why it happens

Green mold, blue mold and sour rot are among the main diseases of oranges after harvest. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss a moldy orange. Citrus is soft, juicy fruit, so mold can spread below the surface. Check the fruit that was touching it.

Prevent it next time
  • Check the bag often and remove any soft orange
  • Don't seal oranges in an airtight bag

Shriveled peel around the stem

AgeStill good

What you'll seeThe peel around the stem end dries, sinks and turns brown, and the orange feels lighter.

Why it happens

As an orange ages, the peel around the stem dries out and breaks down. UC Davis calls it stem-end rind breakdown. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the fruit inside is juicy and smells fresh, with no mold.

Prevent it next time
  • Use oranges within three to four weeks
  • Keep them in the fridge rather than on the counter
Use it up
  • Juice it

Pitted or brown-stained peel

Too coldStill good

What you'll seeSmall sunken pits or brown stains on the peel after cold storage.

Why it happens

Some oranges get chilling injury in the cold: pits and brown stains on the peel, and more decay afterward. How cold is too cold depends on the variety and where it was grown. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the orange is firm and has no mold. Peel it and eat it soon.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep oranges in the crisper drawer rather than against the cold back wall
  • Use them within three to four weeks
Use it up
  • Juice it or segment it into a salad

Oranges and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Oranges give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Oranges are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep them apart from apples.

The part people throw away

About 27% of oranges by weight is usually trimmed off as peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Dry the zest for baking or tea
  • Soak peels in vinegar for 2 weeks for a citrus cleaner
  • Candy the peels
