How to store persimmons, and why they go bad

There are two kinds, and they fail differently. Squat Fuyu are eaten firm and go soft around ethylene. Acorn-shaped Hachiya are not ready until they are jelly-soft.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
1-3 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas, pears and avocados

How to store persimmons

  • Keep firm, squat Fuyu persimmons in the coldest part of the fridge and eat them while they are still crisp
  • Ripen acorn-shaped Hachiya persimmons on the counter until they feel like jelly. They are mouth-puckering until then
  • Keep them away from apples, pears and bananas unless you want them to soften faster
  • Scoop out and freeze the pulp of very soft persimmons for baking

Picking good persimmons: Deep orange, glossy skin with the green cap still attached. Fuyu should be firm. Hachiya should be very soft before you eat it.

Why persimmons go bad

Firm persimmons going soft

Ethylene gasStill good

What you'll seeA crisp Fuyu turns soft within days.

Why it happens

UC Davis rates persimmons as very sensitive to ethylene. At room temperature, 1 part per million softens them past the point stores can sell them in 6 days, and 10 parts per million does it in 2. 1

Is it still OK?

A soft persimmon is safe and sweet. Toss it if it smells fermented or is moldy.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep Fuyu persimmons away from apples, pears and bananas
  • Keep them in the coldest part of the fridge
Use it up
  • Eat it with a spoon
  • Use the pulp in bread, muffins or smoothies

Chalky, mouth-puckering taste

OverripeningStill good

What you'll seeThe fruit looks ripe but dries out your mouth when you bite it.

Why it happens

Hachiya-type persimmons are astringent until they are completely soft. This is an unripe fruit, not a spoiled one. 12

Is it still OK?

Not harmful, just unpleasant. Give it more time on the counter.

Prevent it next time
  • Ripen Hachiya on the counter until they feel like a water balloon
  • Put them in a paper bag with an apple to speed it up
Use it up
  • Once soft, scoop out the pulp and freeze it for baking

Brown, mushy flesh after cold storage

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeA Fuyu looks fine outside but the flesh is brown and soft.

Why it happens

UC Davis says Fuyu-type persimmons get chilling injury between 41 and 59°F, with flesh browning and softening, and that ethylene makes it worse. Colder than that, near freezing, is safe. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if it smells fine and has no mold. The texture suffers, so bake with it.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep Fuyu in the coldest part of the fridge, not the door
  • Eat them within a few weeks
Use it up
  • Use it in bread or pudding

Mold on the skin or under the cap

MoldToss it

What you'll seeBlack or fuzzy spots on the skin or around the green cap.

Why it happens

Mold gets in through cracks and at the cap, and it spreads quickly in soft, ripe fruit. 13

Is it still OK?

Toss a moldy persimmon. It is a soft fruit, so mold reaches past what you can see.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them dry
  • Use ripe persimmons within a day or two

What went wrong?

Persimmons and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Persimmons give off only a little ethylene, so they are fine next to most other produce.
  • Persimmons are very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast they age.

Keep them apart from apples, bananas, pears and avocados.

The part people throw away

About 16% of persimmons by weight is usually trimmed off as cap, skin and seeds (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Fuyu skin is edible: eat it like an apple
  • Dry thin, skin-on slices in a low oven for chips
