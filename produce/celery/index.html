Why it goes bad

Celery: what went wrong, and what to do

Celery mostly fails by drying out. It's less sensitive to ethylene than most greens, and when it does rot, it starts at the cut ends.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
2-4 weeks (refrigerated)
Keep it away from
Apples and Pears
What are you seeing?

Limp, bendy stalks

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeStalks bend instead of snapping.

Why it happens

Celery keeps best at very high humidity, close to 100%. Fridge air is much drier, so uncovered celery wilts. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. Trim the base and stand the stalks in cold water for an hour.

Prevent it next time
  • Wrap it in aluminum foil, or keep cut stalks in a jar of water
  • Store it whole in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Add it to soup, stock or stir-fry

Stalks losing their green color

Ethylene gasStill good

What you'll seeStalks fade from green to pale or yellowish.

Why it happens

Celery isn't very sensitive to ethylene when it's cold. At higher levels and warmer temperatures, though, it loses its green color. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. The color is cosmetic.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep it away from apples and pears
  • Keep it cold
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp helps a little here. Helps the stalks keep their green color. Expect about 1–2 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Hollow, spongy or white inside

AgeCook it soon

What you'll seeThe inside of the stalk is white, dry and spongy instead of solid.

Why it happens

This is pith breakdown, linked to age, cold stress and water stress, sometimes starting before harvest. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. The texture is poor raw but fine in cooking.

Prevent it next time
  • Use older bunches first
Use it up
  • Chop it into soup, stock or a soffritto

Slimy ends or moldy stalks

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeCut ends turn brown and slimy, or stalks show fuzzy gray or white mold.

Why it happens

Cut stalks are especially prone to bacterial rot. Gray mold and watery rot can develop over a few weeks even in a cold fridge. 1

Is it still OK?

Toss slimy or moldy stalks. Other stalks from the bunch are fine if they're firm and smell normal.

Prevent it next time
  • Store celery whole, and cut it when you need it
  • Change the water every couple of days if you keep cut stalks in water

How to store celery

  • Wrap in aluminum foil — this allows ethylene to escape while retaining moisture
  • Store whole in the crisper drawer
  • Cut celery can be stored in water to keep crisp
  • Keep away from ethylene producers

Full storage details are in the Produce Storage Guide.

The part people throw away

About 11% of celery by weight is usually trimmed off as leaves and base (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Use the leaves like parsley in salads and soups
  • Set the base in a dish of water to regrow
