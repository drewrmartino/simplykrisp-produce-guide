How to store winter squash, and why it goes bad

Whole winter squash keeps for a month or more in a cool, dry pantry. Cold damages the rind, damaged rind lets rot in, and very old squash turns dry and stringy.

Where to keep it
Cool, dark pantry
How long it lasts
1-3 months (pantry)

About 54°F (12°C)

How to store winter squash

  • Store in a cool, dark, dry place with good ventilation
  • Do not refrigerate whole squash
  • Once cut, wrap and refrigerate for up to 5 days
  • Keep each squash from touching to prevent rot from spreading

Picking good winter squash: Hard skin that can't be dented with a fingernail, heavy for its size, with a dry, corky stem.

Does a Krisp help? Winter squash keeps for months in a cool pantry, well away from the fridge.

Why winter squash goes bad

Sunken pits in the rind

Too coldTrim and use

What you'll seeSunken pits on the rind, often followed by rot once the squash warms up.

Why it happens

Winter squash gets chilling injury below about 50 to 55°F. A month at 41°F is enough. The rind pits, and the squash decays quickly after it comes out of the cold. 1

Is it still OK?

Cut it open. If the flesh under the pits is firm and smells fresh, trim the pitted areas and cook it soon. Toss it if the flesh is soft, wet or moldy.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep whole squash in a cool pantry, not the fridge
  • Don't store it in an unheated garage or porch in cold weather
Use it up
  • Roast it
  • Cube and freeze it

Soft or moldy spots on the rind

MoldTrim and use

What you'll seeA soft, sunken or discolored patch on the rind, sometimes with fuzz.

Why it happens

Several fungi cause storage rot in winter squash. Squash with a damaged rind or cold damage gets it first. 12

Is it still OK?

Whole winter squash is hard and dense, so USDA's rule for firm vegetables applies: cut off a small mold spot with at least an inch around and below it. If the rot is soft and wet, has reached the seed cavity, or smells bad, toss the squash.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep squash from touching each other
  • Check stored squash every week or two, and use any with a damaged rind first
Use it up
  • Cook the trimmed squash the same day

Dry, stringy flesh

AgeCook it soon

What you'll seeThe squash feels light, and the flesh is dry, stringy or pale.

Why it happens

Squash slowly loses weight and texture in storage, and warm rooms speed it up. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no mold, soft rot or off smell.

Prevent it next time
  • Store squash somewhere cool and dry, with air around it
  • Use the oldest squash first
Use it up
  • Roast it and blend it into soup
  • Steam and mash it

What went wrong?

Winter squash and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Winter squash gives off only a little ethylene, so it is fine next to most other produce.
  • Winter squash is somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing it away from ripening fruit helps.

The part people throw away

About 29% of winter squash by weight is usually trimmed off as seeds and rind (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Rinse, dry and roast the seeds with oil and salt
  • Simmer strings and peels into stock
