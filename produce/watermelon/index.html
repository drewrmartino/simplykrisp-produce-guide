How to store watermelon, and why it goes bad

Whole watermelon keeps best at cool room temperature, not in the fridge. It turns mealy around ripening fruit, loses color and flavor in the cold, and spoils quickly once cut.

Where to keep it
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long it lasts
1-2 weeks whole, 3-5 days cut (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 50°F (10°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas and peaches
What are you seeing?
Mealy, mushy fleshPale, bland flesh after the fridgeCut watermelon turned slimy or sour

How to store watermelon

  • Store whole watermelon on the counter until cut
  • Once cut, wrap tightly and refrigerate
  • Keep away from ethylene-producing fruits
  • Whole melons keep better at cool room temperature than in the fridge

Picking good watermelon: A ripe watermelon sounds hollow when tapped, has a creamy yellow ground spot, and feels heavy for its size.

Does a Krisp help? Whole melons are too big for the drawer. Once cut, wrap it and eat it within a few days.

Why watermelon goes bad

Mealy, mushy flesh

Ethylene gasCook it soon

What you'll seeThe flesh is grainy and soft instead of crisp.

Why it happens

Watermelon makes very little ethylene but reacts badly to it. UC Davis reports that 5 parts per million for a week at 64°F causes an unacceptable loss of firmness and eating quality. Hard knocks do something similar: internal bruising makes the flesh break down early and turn mealy. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if it smells fresh and sweet. The texture is better blended. Toss it if it smells sour or fizzes.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep whole watermelon away from apples, bananas, peaches and other ripening fruit
  • Carry it and set it down gently
Use it up
  • Blend it into juice, agua fresca or a slushie
  • Freeze cubes for smoothies

Pale, bland flesh after the fridge

Too coldStill good

What you'll seeSmall pits in the rind, with flesh that has lost some of its color and flavor.

Why it happens

Whole watermelon is damaged by several days below about 45°F. The rind pits, the flesh loses color and flavor, and it decays faster once it comes back to room temperature. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if it smells fresh. It just won't taste as sweet.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep whole watermelon on the counter or in a cool room
  • Chill it for a day before serving rather than for a week
Use it up
  • Toss it with lime, salt and chili
  • Blend it into a drink

Cut watermelon turned slimy or sour

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeCut pieces feel slick, look dull and smell sour, or the juice fizzes.

Why it happens

Cut watermelon is wet and exposed, so bacteria and yeasts can grow on it. FDA notes that a knife can carry dirt and bacteria from the rind onto the flesh. 21

Is it still OK?

Toss it. Don't taste cut melon that is slimy, fizzy or smells off.

Prevent it next time
  • Scrub the rind under running water before you cut it
  • Wrap cut watermelon tightly and refrigerate it right away
  • Eat cut watermelon within three to five days

What went wrong?

Watermelon and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Watermelon gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Watermelon is very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast it ages.

Keep it apart from apples, bananas and peaches.

The part people throw away

About 48% of watermelon by weight is usually trimmed off as rind (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Pickle the white rind
  • Stir-fry or curry the peeled rind like squash
  • Blend the white rind into agua fresca
