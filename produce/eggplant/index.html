How to store eggplant, and why it goes bad

Eggplant is a warm-weather crop that doesn't like the fridge. It pits and browns in the cold, wrinkles as it dries out, and browns at the cap around ethylene. Use it within a few days.

Where to keep it

5-7 days (refrigerated)

How to store eggplant

  • Store in the crisper drawer without a bag for air circulation
  • Use within a few days for best quality
  • Sensitive to cold — keep in the warmer part of the fridge
  • Do not cut until ready to cook to prevent browning

Picking good eggplant: Firm with smooth, glossy, deep purple skin. Should feel heavy for its size.

Why eggplant goes bad

Pitted skin and brown seeds

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seePits and bronze patches on the skin, with brown seeds and flesh inside.

Why it happens

Eggplant is damaged below 50°F. At 41°F the injury shows in six to eight days, and it adds up over time. Most fridges run around 37°F to 40°F. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if it's firm and smells fresh, with no mold. Cut away any badly browned flesh and cook it soon.

Prevent it next time
  • Use eggplant within a few days of buying it
  • Keep it in the warmest part of the fridge
Use it up
  • Roast it and blend it into a dip
  • Cook it into a curry or stew

Wrinkled skin and spongy flesh

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeThe skin loses its shine and wrinkles, and the flesh feels spongy.

Why it happens

These are the signs of water loss. UC Davis notes that long, thin Japanese eggplants lose water three times faster than the large globe type. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no mold or soft rot. The texture is better cooked down.

Prevent it next time
  • Use it within five to seven days
  • Keep it in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Roast it and mash it into a dip
  • Dice it into a pasta sauce

Brown cap and early browning

Ethylene gasStill good

What you'll seeThe green cap turns brown and may come loose, and the eggplant browns sooner than it should.

Why it happens

Above about 1 part per million, ethylene can make the cap drop off and speeds up deterioration, browning in particular. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the eggplant is firm and the flesh looks normal. Cut off the cap and cook it soon.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep it away from apples, bananas and tomatoes
  • Use it within a few days
Use it up
  • Slice and roast or grill it
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp helps a little here. Slows browning at the cap and softening. Expect about 1–2 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Soft, sunken spots or mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeSoft, sunken, dark patches, or gray, black or white fuzz.

Why it happens

Several molds rot eggplant, including gray mold, black mold and Rhizopus. Eggplant also bruises easily when it's stacked or handled roughly. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it. Eggplant flesh is soft and spongy, so mold can spread below the surface.

Prevent it next time
  • Don't stack anything on it
  • Don't wash it until you cook it

What went wrong?

Eggplant and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Eggplant gives off only a little ethylene, so it is fine next to most other produce.
  • Eggplant is somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing it away from ripening fruit helps.

The part people throw away

About 19% of eggplant by weight is usually trimmed off as ends and peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Bake peels with oil and salt into crispy chips
  • Compost the stem ends
