How to store nectarines, and why they go bad

Nectarines behave like peaches. A fridge turns unripe ones mealy, ripe ones go soft within days, and rot moves fast.

Where to keep them
Counter, then fridge once ripe
How long they last
3-5 days ripe (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 31°F (-1°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas and leafy greens
What are you seeing?
Mealy, flavorless fleshWrinkled and very softBrown soft rot or fuzzy mold

How to store nectarines

  • Ripen at room temperature stem-side down
  • Once ripe, refrigerate immediately
  • Store in a single layer to prevent bruising
  • Keep away from other ethylene producers

Picking good nectarines: Slightly soft along the seam with a fragrant aroma. Avoid green-tinged fruit.

Does a Krisp help? Tested directly: continuous ethylene had no effect on nectarines in cold storage.

Why nectarines go bad

Mealy, flavorless flesh

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeThe flesh is dry and grainy, sometimes brown or streaked red, and the fruit never gets juicy.

Why it happens

Nectarines held at 36 to 46°F are prone to internal breakdown, a kind of chilling injury, and most fridges sit in that range. The damage shows when the fruit ripens: mealy or brown flesh, red bleeding and little flavor. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no mold. Cooking is the best use.

Prevent it next time
  • Ripen nectarines on the counter, stem side down
  • Refrigerate them only once ripe, and eat them within three to five days
Use it up
  • Grill or roast them
  • Cook them into a crisp or a jam

Wrinkled and very soft

OverripeningCook it soon

What you'll seeThe skin wrinkles and the fruit feels squishy all over.

Why it happens

Nectarines keep ripening on the counter. Ripe fruit at room temperature gives off many times more ethylene than unripe fruit does. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if it smells sweet and there's no mold or leaking. Use it today.

Prevent it next time
  • Move ripe nectarines to the fridge
  • Keep them in a single layer
Use it up
  • Blend them into a smoothie
  • Simmer them into a sauce for yogurt or pancakes

Brown soft rot or fuzzy mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft brown spot that spreads quickly, often with tan, gray or white fuzz.

Why it happens

Brown rot, gray mold and Rhizopus rot are the main diseases of nectarines after harvest. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss it. USDA lists peaches as soft fruit, where mold can spread below the surface, and nectarines are the same.

Prevent it next time
  • Remove any rotting fruit right away and check the ones next to it
  • Don't wash nectarines until you eat them

What went wrong?

Nectarines and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Nectarines give off a moderate amount of ethylene as they ripen, enough to speed up sensitive produce in a closed drawer.
  • Nectarines are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep them apart from apples, bananas and leafy greens.

The part people throw away

About 9% of nectarines by weight is usually trimmed off as pits (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Simmer skins with sugar for a quick syrup
  • Compost the pits. Never crack or eat the kernels
