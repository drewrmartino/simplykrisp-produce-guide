How to store lemons, and why they go bad

Lemons keep for weeks. When they fail, it's usually one of three things: they dry out, the peel pits in the cold, or one goes moldy and the fruit around it follows.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
3-4 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 54°F (12°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?

How to store lemons

  • Store in the refrigerator in a sealed bag to prevent drying out
  • Can be left on the counter for about a week
  • Zest before juicing for maximum use
  • Store cut lemons face-down on a plate or in a sealed container

Picking good lemons: Choose bright yellow, firm lemons that feel heavy for their size.

Does a Krisp help? Ethylene de-greens lemons rather than spoiling them, and the baseline life is already months.

Why lemons go bad

Hard, shrunken lemons

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe peel turns hard, thin and dull, and the lemon feels light.

Why it happens

Lemons lose water through the peel. They keep best at 90 to 95 percent humidity, and both a fruit bowl and an open fridge shelf are drier than that. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if there's no mold. There is just less juice.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep lemons in a sealed bag in the fridge
Use it up
  • Microwave it for 10 seconds, roll it, then juice it
  • Zest it and freeze the zest

Soft spot, then green or blue powder

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft, watery patch on the peel that grows white fuzz, then a dusty green or blue coating.

Why it happens

These are green mold and blue mold, the main diseases of citrus after harvest. They start as a water-soaked spot, then the spores appear. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss a moldy lemon. Citrus is soft, juicy fruit, so mold can spread below the surface. Check the fruit that was touching it.

Prevent it next time
  • Check the bag or bowl often and remove any soft lemon
  • Handle lemons gently. Cuts and scratches are where decay starts

Pitted or stained peel

Too coldStill good

What you'll seeSmall sunken pits or reddish-brown stains on the peel after a long stay in the fridge.

Why it happens

Lemons keep best at 54 to 57°F, warmer than a fridge. After a long time in the cold the peel can pit and stain, and badly damaged fruit decays more easily. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if the lemon is firm and has no mold. The damage is in the peel, so juice it.

Prevent it next time
  • Buy what you'll use in three to four weeks
  • Use the oldest lemons first
Use it up
  • Juice it and freeze the juice in an ice cube tray

What went wrong?

Lemons and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Lemons give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Lemons are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

The part people throw away

About 47% of lemons by weight is usually trimmed off as peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Zest before juicing and freeze the zest
  • Soak spent peels in vinegar for 2 weeks for a citrus cleaner
  • Candy the peels in simple syrup
