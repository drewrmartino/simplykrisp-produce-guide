How to store okra, and why it goes bad

Okra is one of the most perishable vegetables, two or three days at home. It bruises, dries out, and is damaged by a cold fridge.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
2-3 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 47°F (8°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Brown or black ridgesDark, pitted or water-soaked spotsLimp, tough podsSlimy or moldy pods

How to store okra

  • Keep the pods dry in a paper bag or a loosely closed bag
  • Store them in the warmest part of the fridge, like the door or the front of the crisper
  • Do not wash them until just before cooking. Wet pods turn slimy
  • Use them within 2 to 3 days, or slice and freeze them

Picking good okra: Small, bright green pods, 2 to 4 inches long, that snap at the tip. Big, dull pods are tough and stringy.

Does a Krisp help? Okra lasts only two or three days at home. Drying out, bruising and cold damage end it long before ethylene matters, so a Krisp won't help.

Why okra goes bad

Brown or black ridges

BruisingStill good

What you'll seeThe ridges and tips of the pods turn brown or black.

Why it happens

UC Davis says tender okra pods are easily damaged, especially on the ridges, and the damage turns into brown and black discoloration. 1

Is it still OK?

Cosmetic. The pods are fine if they are still firm and not slimy.

Prevent it next time
  • Handle the pods gently
  • Do not pack it tightly or under other produce
Use it up
  • Roast or fry it
  • Add it to gumbo or stew

Dark, pitted or water-soaked spots

Too coldTrim and use

What you'll seePods get dark sunken pits or wet-looking patches, then rot.

Why it happens

Okra keeps best at 45 to 50°F, warmer than most fridges. UC Davis lists discoloration, pitting, water-soaked spots and more decay as chilling injury. 1

Is it still OK?

Cut away pitted spots and cook the pods the same day. Toss any that are soft, slimy or moldy.

Prevent it next time
  • Store okra in the warmest part of the fridge, like the door
  • Use it within 2 to 3 days
Use it up
  • Slice and sauté it

Limp, tough pods

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seePods lose their snap and turn dull, limp or woody.

Why it happens

UC Davis notes that young okra pods lose water very fast, and that it takes very high humidity to slow drying, toughening and loss of freshness. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there is no slime or mold. Tough pods need longer, wetter cooking.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the pods in a loosely closed bag
  • Buy okra the day you plan to cook it, or the day before
Use it up
  • Stew it with tomatoes

Slimy or moldy pods

Trapped moistureToss it

What you'll seeThe outside of the pods turns slimy or grows fuzz.

Why it happens

Wet pods rot quickly. UC Davis says decay that follows chilling or injury is probably the most common cause of loss. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss okra that is slimy on the outside or moldy.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the pods dry and do not wash them until you cook them

What went wrong?

Okra and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Okra gives off only a little ethylene, so it is fine next to most other produce.
  • Okra is somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing it away from ripening fruit helps.

The part people throw away

About 14% of okra by weight is usually trimmed off as caps (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Compost the caps
  • Slice and freeze extra pods before they turn
