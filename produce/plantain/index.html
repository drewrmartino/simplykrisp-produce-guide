How to store plantains, and why they go bad

Plantains are used at every stage from green to black, so most of what looks like spoiling is ripening. Cold is what really damages them.

Where to keep them
Counter
How long they last
1-2 weeks (counter)
Best temperature
About 57°F (14°C)
Don't store next to
Apples, bananas, avocados and tomatoes
What are you seeing?
Peel turning yellow, then blackDull, gray-brown peel that will not ripenBrown, bruised fleshMold at the stem or tip

How to store plantains

  • Keep them on the counter, out of direct sun. Never refrigerate green plantains
  • Green ones are starchy, for tostones. Yellow with black spots are sweet, for frying
  • To speed up ripening, put them in a paper bag
  • Once fully ripe, refrigerate them for a few days or peel and freeze them

Picking good plantains: Firm and heavy with no mold at the stem. Choose green for savory dishes and yellow to black for sweet ones.

Does a Krisp help? Plantains belong on the counter, and a Krisp works inside the closed crisper drawer, so it can't reach them. Keep them away from apples and bananas if you want them to stay green.

Why plantains go bad

Peel turning yellow, then black

OverripeningStill good

What you'll seeA green plantain turns yellow, then spotted, then almost fully black.

Why it happens

This is ripening. Starch turns to sugar as the peel darkens, and ethylene from other fruit speeds it up. 1

Is it still OK?

A black plantain is ripe, not rotten, as long as it is not moldy, leaking or sour-smelling. It is the sweetest stage for frying.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep green plantains away from apples and bananas if you want them to stay green
  • Cook them at the stage your recipe calls for
Use it up
  • Fry ripe ones as maduros
  • Bake very ripe ones or mash them into batter

Dull, gray-brown peel that will not ripen

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeThe peel turns dull, smoky or brown and the fruit stays hard.

Why it happens

UC Davis lists a dull or smoky peel, browning and failure to ripen as chilling injury in plantains kept at 45°F or colder for about a week. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there is no mold. It will not sweeten, so cook it as a green plantain.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep green plantains on the counter, never in the fridge
  • Only refrigerate plantains that are already fully ripe
Use it up
  • Boil it or fry it as tostones

Brown, bruised flesh

BruisingTrim and use

What you'll seeBrown patches in the flesh, sometimes with no mark on the peel.

Why it happens

UC Davis notes that dropping a plantain can brown the flesh with or without damage to the skin, and that the damaged spots can then mold. 12

Is it still OK?

Cut away the bruised part and use the rest. Toss it if the bruise is moldy or smells fermented.

Prevent it next time
  • Handle them gently
  • Do not stack heavy items on them
Use it up
  • Fry or boil the rest

Mold at the stem or tip

MoldToss it

What you'll seeFuzzy mold at the stem end, or a dry, dark, rotted tip.

Why it happens

UC Davis lists crown rot, stem-end rot and cigar-end rot as the main plantain diseases. They start at the cut stem or the tip and grow into the fruit. 13

Is it still OK?

Toss a plantain if mold or rot has reached the flesh. A little surface mold on the dry stem of an otherwise firm plantain peels away with the skin.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them dry, with air around them
  • Use ripe plantains within a few days

What went wrong?

Plantains and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Plantains give off only a little ethylene, so they are fine next to most other produce.
  • Plantains are very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast they age.

Keep them apart from apples, bananas, avocados and tomatoes.

The part people throw away

About 35% of plantains by weight is usually trimmed off as peel (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Compost the peels
  • Peel and freeze ripe plantains you will not cook this week
