How to store radishes, and why they go bad

Radishes keep a week or two. They go rubbery as they dry out, the tops yellow well before the roots give up, and warm storage brings on dark spots.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
1-2 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Soft, rubbery radishesYellow, wilted topsDark spots or soft rot

How to store radishes

  • Remove greens before storing — they draw moisture
  • Store in a plastic bag in the crisper drawer
  • Keep in water for extra crispness
  • Greens can be stored separately and used within a day or two

Picking good radishes: Firm with smooth, brightly colored skin and fresh-looking greens.

Does a Krisp help? The roots are not ethylene-sensitive; the tops are. Twist the tops off and the problem goes away.

Why radishes go bad

Soft, rubbery radishes

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe radishes feel soft and bendy instead of crisp.

Why it happens

Radishes go soft as they lose water. UC Davis recommends 95 to 100 percent humidity and notes that ice on top helps them keep a crisp texture. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good if there are no dark, soft spots and they smell fresh.

Prevent it next time
  • Cut off the greens before storing
  • Keep the roots in a bag in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Soak them in ice water for 30 minutes to an hour to firm them up
  • Slice and roast or sauté them

Yellow, wilted tops

AgeTrim and use

What you'll seeThe leafy tops turn yellow, wilt, and may go slimy.

Why it happens

The tops age faster than the roots. UC Davis notes that they yellow with longer storage and with exposure to ethylene. 1

Is it still OK?

Cut off and discard yellow or slimy tops. The roots are fine if they're firm.

Prevent it next time
  • Cut the tops off when you get home and store them separately
  • Use the greens within a day or two
Use it up
  • Slice the roots into a salad

Dark spots or soft rot

Bacterial rotToss it

What you'll seeBlack or dark spots on the root, gray fuzz, or soft, watery, sunken patches.

Why it happens

Bacterial black spot and other lesions develop when radishes are stored warmer than they should be. Soft rot and gray mold turn the root watery or fuzzy. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss radishes with dark soft spots, slime or mold. USDA allows cutting a small mold spot off firm vegetables with at least an inch around it, and a radish is too small for that. The firm, clean radishes in the bunch are fine.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep radishes cold
  • Keep them in a bag, and dry them off if they are wet

Radishes and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Radishes give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Radishes are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 10% of radishes by weight is usually trimmed off as greens (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Blend the greens into pesto
  • Sauté greens with garlic like spinach
