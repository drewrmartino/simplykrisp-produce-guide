How to store sweet potatoes, and why they go bad

Sweet potatoes are a pantry item. The fridge damages them, a warm kitchen dries them out, and broken skin lets rot in.

Where to keep them
Cool, dark pantry
How long they last
3-5 weeks (pantry)
Best temperature
About 56°F (13°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular
What are you seeing?
Hard center after cookingWrinkled, shriveled endsSoft, moldy or black patches

How to store sweet potatoes

  • Store in a cool, dark, well-ventilated area
  • Do not refrigerate — cold temperatures alter texture and flavor
  • Keep away from heat sources
  • Use within a month for best quality

Picking good sweet potatoes: Firm with smooth, even-colored skin and no cracks or soft spots.

Does a Krisp help? A pantry item that should not be refrigerated at all.

Why sweet potatoes go bad

Hard center after cooking

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeParts of the sweet potato stay hard however long it's cooked, and the flesh looks darker than usual.

Why it happens

Sweet potatoes get chilling injury at 55°F or below, so a fridge damages them. UC Davis calls the result hardcore: cooked roots with a hard center and darker color. Chilled roots can also brown inside, shrivel and rot. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no mold, rot or off smell. Some parts may never soften.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep sweet potatoes in a cool, dark pantry, not the fridge
  • Use them within about a month
Use it up
  • Cut it into small cubes and simmer it in soup or stew
  • Mash the parts that soften

Wrinkled, shriveled ends

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeThe ends shrivel and the skin wrinkles.

Why it happens

Sweet potatoes lose water in storage. They lose it faster where the skin is scraped or broken, and faster above about 59°F. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if it's still firm, with no mold or soft, wet spots. Cut off the dried ends.

Prevent it next time
  • Handle them gently. The skin is thin
  • Keep them away from the stove and other warm spots
Use it up
  • Roast it
  • Peel, cube and simmer it in soup

Soft, moldy or black patches

MoldToss it

What you'll seeSoft, wet patches, white or gray fuzz, or dark sunken spots.

Why it happens

Rhizopus soft rot, black rot and Fusarium rot all attack stored sweet potatoes. Broken skin and cold damage both make decay more likely. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss a sweet potato with soft, wet or moldy patches or an off smell. FDA says to throw away produce that looks rotten.

Prevent it next time
  • Use sweet potatoes with scraped or broken skin first
  • Don't wash them until you cook them

Sweet potatoes and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Sweet potatoes give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Sweet potatoes are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 28% of sweet potatoes by weight is usually trimmed off as peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Leave the skin on when roasting
  • Bake peels with oil and salt into crisps
