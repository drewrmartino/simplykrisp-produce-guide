How to store corn, and why it goes bad

Sweet corn is at its best the day it's picked. After that the sugar turns to starch, the husks dry out, and old ears mold. Eat it within a day or three.

Where to keep it
Fridge
How long it lasts
1-3 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular

How to store corn

  • Keep in the husk until ready to cook
  • Refrigerate immediately — corn loses sweetness rapidly
  • Use as soon as possible after purchase
  • Store in the crisper drawer without removing silk

Picking good corn: Look for bright green, tightly wrapped husks with moist silk. Kernels should be plump when peeked.

Does a Krisp help? Sweet corn loses its sugar to starch conversion, which is a temperature problem. Chill it fast instead.

Why corn goes bad

Starchy, bland kernels

AgeCook it soon

What you'll seeThe kernels are tough and doughy, and the corn isn't sweet.

Why it happens

Corn turns its sugar into starch after harvest. UC Davis says standard sweet corn isn't stored for more than a few days because quality drops quickly, even at ideal temperatures. Warmth makes it faster. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no mold, slime or sour smell. It just isn't sweet.

Prevent it next time
  • Refrigerate corn as soon as you get home
  • Eat it within one to three days
  • Keep it in the husk until you cook it
Use it up
  • Cut the kernels off for chowder, cornbread or fritters
  • Sauté the kernels with butter and salt

Dry, papery husks

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeThe husks turn pale, dry and papery, and the silk dries out.

Why it happens

Corn loses moisture after picking. UC Davis notes that the stalk ends are trimmed short to limit it. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if the kernels look normal and there's no mold or sour smell. The ear is past its best.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the husks on
  • Store the ears in a bag in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Cut the kernels off and add them to soup or a stir-fry

Mold, slime or a sour smell

MoldToss it

What you'll seeFuzzy mold on the silk or the tip of the ear, or kernels that are slimy or smell sour.

Why it happens

UC Davis notes that the dry silk often grows surface mold once corn has been stored for more than about 10 days. Corn that old is well past its best. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss an ear with mold, slime or a sour smell. Corn kernels are soft and moist, so mold can spread below the surface.

Prevent it next time
  • Buy corn close to when you plan to eat it
  • Keep it cold and dry

What went wrong?

Corn and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Corn gives off almost no ethylene, so it is safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Corn is barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 64% of corn by weight is usually trimmed off as cobs and husks (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Simmer cobs 45 minutes for a sweet corn stock (great for chowder)
  • Use husks to wrap fish or tamales on the grill
