How to store parsnips, and why they go bad

Parsnips keep for weeks. When they fail it is usually a bitter taste from ethylene, or going limp as they dry out.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
2-3 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples and pears
What are you seeing?
Bitter tasteLimp, rubbery parsnipsBrown patches on the skinMoldy or slimy spots

How to store parsnips

  • Cut off any green tops. They pull moisture out of the root
  • Store unwashed in a plastic bag in the crisper drawer
  • Keep them away from apples and pears. Ethylene makes parsnips bitter
  • Peel or scrub them just before cooking

Picking good parsnips: Firm, cream-colored roots, small to medium in size. Very large ones can have a woody core.

Why parsnips go bad

Bitter taste

Ethylene gasCook it soon

What you'll seeThe parsnips look fine but taste bitter.

Why it happens

USDA's storage handbook says even low levels of ethylene in cold storage make parsnips bitter. An apple or pear in the same drawer is a source. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat. Cooking them with something sweet balances the bitterness.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep parsnips away from apples, pears and other fruit
  • Store them in a bag in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Roast them with honey or maple
  • Blend them into soup with apple or carrot
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp helps a little here. Helps keep parsnips from picking up a bitter taste from ethylene. Expect about 1–2 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Limp, rubbery parsnips

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe roots bend instead of snapping and may look shriveled.

Why it happens

Parsnips go rubbery when they lose water. Commercial storage keeps them at 98 percent humidity. 1

Is it still OK?

Still good. Soak them in cold water for an hour to firm them back up.

Prevent it next time
  • Cut off any green tops
  • Keep them in a closed bag in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Roast them
  • Simmer them in soup or stew

Brown patches on the skin

BruisingStill good

What you'll seeTan or brown areas on the surface of the root.

Why it happens

USDA's storage handbook ties surface browning in parsnips mostly to bruising and scuffing, and notes it increases the longer they are stored. 1

Is it still OK?

Cosmetic. Peel them and they are fine, as long as the flesh is firm.

Prevent it next time
  • Handle them gently
  • Use older parsnips first
Use it up
  • Peel and roast them

Moldy or slimy spots

MoldTrim and use

What you'll seeGray fuzz, or soft, wet, sunken patches.

Why it happens

Gray mold, bacterial soft rot and watery soft rot all attack stored parsnips. Damaged roots get them first. 12

Is it still OK?

Parsnips are firm, so USDA says a small mold spot can be cut off with at least an inch around and below it. Toss one that is slimy or soft all the way through.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep the bag free of standing water
  • Check stored parsnips every week or so

What went wrong?

Parsnips and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Parsnips give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Parsnips are very sensitive to ethylene. Even a little in a closed drawer speeds up how fast they age.

Keep them apart from apples and pears.

The part people throw away

About 15% of parsnips by weight is usually trimmed off as peels (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Scrub instead of peeling when they are young
  • Toss the peels in oil and salt and roast them into crisps
