How to store cranberries, and why they go bad

Fresh cranberries are tough for a berry and keep for weeks. Sort them before you cook: soft or brown ones go, firm ones stay.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
3-4 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 36°F (2°C)
Don't store next to
Nothing in particular

How to store cranberries

  • Store in their original bag in the refrigerator
  • Remove any soft or discolored berries
  • Do not wash until ready to use
  • Freeze for up to a year — they freeze exceptionally well

Picking good cranberries: Firm, plump, and deep red. They should bounce when dropped — soft berries are past their prime.

Does a Krisp help? Low ethylene sensitivity and a long baseline life.

Why cranberries go bad

Soft, brown or leaking berries

AgeTrim and use

What you'll seeSome berries in the bag are soft, brown or wet, while the rest are firm.

Why it happens

Cranberries can be held for two to four months in cold storage, so a bag may not be new when you buy it. Berries that break down go soft and leak, and decay follows. 12

Is it still OK?

Sort them. Toss berries that are soft, brown, leaking or moldy. The firm, glossy ones are fine to use.

Prevent it next time
  • Sort the bag when you get home and remove soft berries
  • Keep them dry and unwashed until you use them
  • Freeze them if you won't use them within a few weeks
Use it up
  • Cook the firm ones into sauce or bake them into bread
  • Freeze the firm ones in their bag

Dull, rubbery berries

Too coldCook it soon

What you'll seeBerries lose their shine and feel rubbery instead of firm.

Why it happens

Cranberries are the one common berry that can get too cold. Below 37°F they can develop chilling injury: a dull look, a rubbery texture and more decay. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if they aren't soft, leaking or moldy. Cook them.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them in the main part of the fridge, not against the back wall
Use it up
  • Simmer them into cranberry sauce

Shriveled berries

Drying outCook it soon

What you'll seeBerries wrinkle and lose their gloss.

Why it happens

Water loss makes cranberries shrivel and lose their shine. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe if there's no mold or leaking. They are fine cooked.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them in their original bag in the fridge
Use it up
  • Cook them into sauce, chutney or baked goods

Cranberries and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Cranberries give off only a little ethylene, so they are fine next to most other produce.
  • Cranberries are barely affected by ethylene.

The part people throw away

About 2% of cranberries by weight is usually trimmed off as stems and soft berries (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Cook soft berries into sauce
  • Compost the stems
