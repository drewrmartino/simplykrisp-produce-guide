How to store mandarins, and why they go bad

Mandarins keep a week or two in the fridge. Mold is the usual ending, and one bad fruit in the bag spreads it to the rest.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
1-2 weeks (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 43°F (6°C)
Don't store next to
Apples
What are you seeing?
Green or blue moldDry, shriveled fruitPits or brown patches on the peel

How to store mandarins

  • Refrigerate loose in the crisper drawer if you will not eat them within a few days
  • Take them out of the mesh bag or box so one moldy fruit does not spread to the rest
  • A few days in a fruit bowl is fine for the ones you will eat first
  • Check every few days and remove any soft or moldy fruit

Picking good mandarins: Heavy for their size with glossy, deep orange skin. A slightly loose peel is normal. Soft spots or a musty smell are not.

Does a Krisp help? Citrus reacts only mildly to ethylene, and mold is what usually ends mandarins. A Krisp won't change much.

Why mandarins go bad

Green or blue mold

MoldToss it

What you'll seeA soft, wet patch turns white, then powdery green or blue.

Why it happens

Green mold and blue mold are the main decay of mandarins after harvest. The spores spread from fruit to fruit and get in through small breaks in the thin peel. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss a moldy mandarin. It is a soft fruit, so mold reaches past what you can see. Firm, clean neighbors are fine after a rinse.

Prevent it next time
  • Take mandarins out of the bag and check them when you get home
  • Refrigerate them. Cold slows mold down
  • Remove a moldy fruit right away

Dry, shriveled fruit

Drying outStill good

What you'll seeThe peel turns leathery and loose, and the segments inside are dry.

Why it happens

Mandarins have a thin peel and lose water quickly in dry air. Commercial storage holds them at 90 to 95 percent humidity, far above a fruit bowl or an open fridge shelf. 3

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if it smells fine and has no mold. It will be less juicy.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep them in the crisper drawer, not a bowl, if they will sit more than a few days
  • Eat the oldest ones first
Use it up
  • Juice them
  • Zest the peel before it dries out completely

Pits or brown patches on the peel

Too coldTrim and use

What you'll seeSmall sunken pits or brown areas show up on the peel after long cold storage.

Why it happens

UC Davis lists pitting and brown discoloration as chilling injury in mandarins held below 41°F, getting worse the longer and colder they are kept, and followed by more decay. A week or two in a home fridge rarely causes it. 1

Is it still OK?

Peel it. If the fruit inside looks and smells normal, it is fine. Toss it if it is soft, moldy or smells off.

Prevent it next time
  • Eat refrigerated mandarins within about two weeks
  • Keep them out of the coldest spot at the back of the fridge
Use it up
  • Eat them soon, or juice them

What went wrong?

Mandarins and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Mandarins give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Mandarins are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep them apart from apples.

The part people throw away

About 26% of mandarins by weight is usually trimmed off as peel and seeds (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • Dry the peels for tea or to scent a simmer pot
  • Zest before peeling and freeze the zest
