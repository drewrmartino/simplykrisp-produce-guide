How to store peas, and why they go bad

Fresh peas lose their sweetness and tenderness within days. The pods also yellow around ethylene, and old or damp ones spot and rot.

Where to keep them
Fridge
How long they last
3-5 days (refrigerated)
Best temperature
About 32°F (0°C)
Don't store next to
Apples and bananas

Yellowing podsStarchy, tough peasSpotted or slimy pods

How to store peas

  • Refrigerate immediately — peas lose sweetness fast
  • Store in a perforated bag in the crisper
  • Do not shell until ready to eat or cook
  • Blanch and freeze for long-term storage

Picking good peas: Bright green, plump pods that are firm to the touch.

Why peas go bad

Yellowing pods

Ethylene gasCook it soon

What you'll seeThe pods fade from bright green to yellow, and the stem end turns brown.

Why it happens

UC Davis notes that long exposure to even low levels of ethylene speeds up yellowing and decay in pea pods. The small leafy cap at the stem end is more sensitive than the pod. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no slime or mold. They are past their best raw.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep peas away from apples, bananas and other ripening fruit
  • Store them in a perforated bag in the crisper drawer
Use it up
  • Stir-fry them
  • Simmer them in soup
Where a Krisp fits

A Krisp helps a little here. Slows the pods yellowing. Expect about 1–2 extra days in a closed crisper drawer. See the Double Krisp

Starchy, tough peas

AgeCook it soon

What you'll seeThe peas taste starchy instead of sweet, and the pods are tough.

Why it happens

Peas age quickly after picking. UC Davis lists yellowing, lost tenderness and starchiness as what goes wrong with time, and all of it happens faster at 41 to 50°F than at 32°F. 1

Is it still OK?

Safe to eat if there's no slime or mold. They are better cooked.

Prevent it next time
  • Refrigerate peas right away
  • Eat them within three to five days
  • Don't shell them until you cook them
Use it up
  • Simmer them into pea soup
  • Mash them with mint and lemon

Spotted or slimy pods

MoldToss it

What you'll seeBrown spots on the pods, gray or white fuzz, or soft, wet, slimy patches.

Why it happens

Several fungi spot and rot pea pods, including gray mold. Bacterial soft rot is common after rough handling. 12

Is it still OK?

Toss pods that are slimy or moldy. Pea pods are soft and moist, so mold can't be cut away. If the rest are dry, firm and smell fresh, wash and cook them today.

Prevent it next time
  • Keep peas dry and unwashed until you use them
  • Handle them gently

What went wrong?

Peas and ethylene

Ethylene is a gas that ripening produce gives off. In a closed crisper drawer it builds up and makes sensitive produce yellow, soften or sprout sooner.

  • Peas give off almost no ethylene, so they are safe to store next to most other produce.
  • Peas are somewhat sensitive to ethylene, so storing them away from ripening fruit helps.

Keep them apart from apples and bananas.

The part people throw away

About 6% of peas by weight is usually trimmed off as ends, strings and pods (USDA FoodData Central). Some of it can still be used:

  • If you shelled peas, simmer the pods for a sweet stock or purée
  • Compost the strings
